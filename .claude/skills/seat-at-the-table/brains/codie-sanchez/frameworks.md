# Frameworks: Codie Sanchez

24 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (choose the game, screen the deal, structure it, protect the downside, run it, sell it, build personal wealth). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that use the framework or a clear synonym of it. Book tags show where a framework sits in her books as she teaches them in public; no book text is used. Quotes come from auto captions, so they keep caption quirks.

## Choose the game

### 1. Buy versus build (odds, not heroics) *(Main Street Millionaire)*
- **What it is:** If you want cash flow rather than a burning idea, buy a profitable business instead of starting one, because the odds are better and the money arrives on day one.
- **Parts:** Startups: about 90% fail over ten years, founders average roughly $40K to $67K a year, three to four years before profit (S030, S048, S064). Acquired small businesses: SBA acquisition loan failure around 13% over ten years, 5% to 25% across cycles (S042, S043, S064); "80% of acquisitions survive the first year" (S049). The Lindy effect: a business five to seven years old has shed roughly half its failure risk (S035, S052). The exception she grants: a dream so big you can't sleep, or a capital-heavy category, belongs in a startup (S014, S056).
- **When to use:** Any "should I start X" decision, a new product line, or a new location.
- **How to score:** Ask whether someone already sells this profitably and whether you could buy it, buy part of it, or buy a team. If yes, building must clear a much higher bar. Building wins only with a real unfair advantage or a mission the founder cannot let go of.
- **Quote:** "I'm just going to go where the odds are better like I just have a better shot at winning if I'm good at poker than I do at playing slots" (S043)
- **Also:** "I know that somebody already wants it cuz they have revenue and it's profitable and I can just help it grow." (S025)
- **Sources:** 26 (S001, S002, S006, S007, S010, S014, S017, S024, S025, S026, S029, S030, S031, S035, S036, S038, S040, S042, S043, S048, S049, S055, S058, S059, S062, S064)

### 2. How, Who, Buy (three levels of problem solving)
- **What it is:** Broke people treat every problem as a how problem; people with some money make it a who problem; the top level asks how to buy the solution with the highest certainty.
- **Parts:** How (do it yourself), Who (hire or partner with someone who can), Buy (acquire a team, a customer base, or a company). Examples: Bezos buying Whole Foods for distribution (S042); buying clients "in chunks" instead of one at a time (S033, S036). She admits she slips back to "how" often (S035).
- **When to use:** Growth plans that assume organic, one-at-a-time effort, or a founder trying to learn a whole new skill.
- **How to score:** For each option, ask whether it can be bought (customers, capacity, talent) faster and more surely than built. Prefer the option with the highest certainty per dollar and per month.
- **Quote:** "A third level entrepreneur thinks how can I buy the solution to this problem with the highest certainty" (S042)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S012, S034, S035, S036, S042, S048)

### 3. Boring versus sexy (narrative versus numbers)
- **What it is:** Money concentrates where the story is unglamorous and the numbers are good. The sexier the industry, the fewer people get rich in it.
- **Parts:** Look for a gap between narrative and numbers (S003, S013). Sexy industries draw crowds and inflated valuations; boring ones (roofing, plumbing, trash, laundromats, painting) pay more people consistently (S035, S053). Compete with the 65-year-old handyman, not Jeff Bezos or HBO (S038, S043, S064).
- **When to use:** Choosing a market, a new line of business, or an acquisition target.
- **How to score:** Count credible, well-funded competitors and check margins. Fewer polished rivals plus steady demand scores high; a hot category with venture-funded rivals scores low.
- **Quote:** "The sexier the industry the fewer the people make an obscene amount of money in it." (S035)
- **Also:** "I always want to go where there's what I call a difference between narrative and numbers." (S003)
- **Sources:** 15 (S003, S005, S013, S019, S024, S026, S035, S038, S043, S045, S049, S051, S053, S059, S064)

## Screen the deal

### 4. Buy box: BRRT and SOWS
- **What it is:** Two quick screens for what she will buy.
- **Parts:** BRRT: Boring, Recession resistant, Raise the prices, add Technology (S017, S024, S043). SOWS: Stale (fax machine test: no tech, no marketing, still profitable), Old (5 to 7+ years, Lindy), Weak (weak competitors and weak execution you can fix), Simple (explainable to grandma or a seven-year-old) (S035, S039, S048). Her own box adds: profitable only, no turnarounds, explainable to her grandmother (S032). Her holding company box: under $50M revenue, at least $1M profit, US service businesses (S042).
- **When to use:** First pass on any acquisition, investment, or new venture.
- **How to score:** Pass or fail on each letter. One "no" on profitable or simple usually kills it for a first deal.
- **Quote:** "We buy boring businesses in recession resistant asset classes raise their prices and add technology" (S043)
- **Also:** "I want it to be stale, old, weak, and simple." (S048)
- **Sources:** 12 (S013, S017, S024, S032, S035, S039, S043, S048, S049, S052, S056, S059)

### 5. Deal clarity, deal box, and the Perfect Fit Venn *(Main Street Millionaire)*
- **What it is:** There is no good or bad deal in the abstract, only a good or bad deal for you. Define your box before you look.
- **Parts:** Perfect Fit Venn: obsession or passion, network, skills (S031, S032, S042). Deal box fields: location, sector, profit level, operate or invest, price range, financing need, required cash flow, must-haves and can't-stands (S037, S049). Three numbers before buying: your own money at risk, what you will borrow or seller-finance, what you want to make (S037). Backdoor math: want $100K, buy at 2 to 3x, so target about $300K profit (S009).
- **When to use:** Before sourcing, and whenever a founder is chasing a deal because someone else did it (mimetic desire, S038).
- **How to score:** Green if inside the box, yellow if two or three things are missing, red if any must-have fails (S024).
- **Quote:** "There's no such thing as a good or B good deal or bad deal just a good deal or bad deal for you." (S037)
- **Also:** "That's how I know you're not ready to buy a business because there is no one type of business to buy" (S042)
- **Sources:** 13 (S009, S015, S019, S023, S024, S031, S032, S034, S037, S038, S042, S049, S058)

### 6. Due diligence in two questions
- **What it is:** All diligence reduces to: are the numbers real, and will they continue?
- **Parts:** Match the P&L to the tax return or lower the price to the tax return figure (S035). Watch add-backs (the truck, the family trip) (S040). Count quarters on random days for cash businesses; secret-shop competitors; discount a claimed 30 to 40% margin to 15 to 20% (S042). Deal team: attorney, accountant, and a sector expert who has owned that kind of business (S031, S048). A ranked due diligence question list (DDQ) and a post-op on every deal that goes wrong (S017). Find the one thing that can kill you, and get a third-party asset valuation (S016).
- **When to use:** Any purchase, investment, or partnership where you are at an information disadvantage.
- **How to score:** If the numbers cannot be verified against tax returns or bank deposits, cut the price or walk.
- **Quote:** "Are the numbers real? Because we buy we don't buy hopes and dreams. We buy realities and cash flow." (S052)
- **Also:** "Every time you buy a small business you're at an information disadvantage." (S040)
- **Sources:** 14 (S005, S013, S015, S016, S017, S019, S024, S031, S035, S040, S042, S048, S052, S065)

## Structure the deal

### 7. Price versus terms
- **What it is:** Price and terms are the only two levers. Amateurs fight over price; pros let the seller have the price and control the terms.
- **Parts:** My price and your terms, or your terms and my price (she credits David Osborne, S024). Extreme example: a billion dollars for a business, paid a dollar a day (S038, S039). Terms include down payment, interest, length, earnouts, milestones, clawbacks, and who gets paid first (S008). Revenue share beats profit share because profit can be engineered down (S038).
- **When to use:** Every negotiation, including vendor contracts, hiring packages, and fundraising.
- **How to score:** Compare options on cash out of pocket, cash flow to you in year one, and downside if it goes wrong, not on headline price.
- **Quote:** "You can have your price and my terms or my terms and your price but you can't have both." (S039)
- **Also:** "The pro knows that you want control of the terms not the price always." (S008)
- **Sources:** 10 (S008, S012, S024, S036, S038, S039, S052, S055, S058, S066)

### 8. Seller financing: time, money, and taxes *(Main Street Millionaire)*
- **What it is:** The seller acts as the bank and is paid from the business's future profits. Her favorite way to buy.
- **Parts:** About 60% of small business sales include some seller financing (S010, S039, S060). Pitch in three yeses: more money (bank values at $750K, she offers $1.15M on a seller note), lower taxes (installment gains spread over years), faster close (weeks instead of the SBA's 90 to 120 days) (S048, S049). Pay the seller 4 to 5% instead of the bank's 8% (S035, S037). Find the "seller financing avatar": older, tired, no successor, unbankable books, simple business, knows you (S026, S049). Trade a higher price for $0 down (S010). Pitching it as a win-win doubled her acceptance rate (S035).
- **When to use:** Any purchase where the founder lacks cash, wants to avoid a personal guarantee, or needs speed.
- **How to score:** Seller note share of price, payment as a percent of monthly profit, and whether the deal still pays if revenue drops 25%.
- **Quote:** "Would you like to sell your business for more, pay the IRS less, and move faster?" (S048)
- **Also:** "Seller financing is just removing the bank. Doesn't mean that you have to have all the money." (S049)
- **Sources:** 24 (S001, S005, S009, S010, S011, S012, S016, S017, S026, S029, S030, S031, S034, S035, S037, S039, S040, S042, S043, S048, S049, S052, S058, S060)

### 9. The three legs: cash, expertise, sweat (the Get Rich Tripod)
- **What it is:** You can buy into a business with money, experience, or time. Money is the highest leverage because it is not finite like time.
- **Parts:** Cash (your own, an SBA loan, investors, a securities-backed loan) (S058). Expertise (an accountant buys into an accounting firm for zero) (S017). Sweat (operate it and earn equity over time) (S017, S048). Matching risks: capital risk, reputational risk, time risk (S056).
- **When to use:** "I can't afford it" objections, and structuring roles between founders, operators, and investors.
- **How to score:** Each party should be paid in proportion to the risk they carry and when they carry it; up-front capital earns more than later sweat.
- **Quote:** "You can buy a business with three things. You can bring the money, you can bring the experience, or you can bring the sweat." (S048)
- **Also:** "Money is your highest leverage like right because it's not finite like time is" (S017)
- **Sources:** 9 (S017, S026, S028, S029, S045, S048, S056, S058, S062)

### 10. Expertise to equity and partial acquisitions
- **What it is:** Get a percentage of a business with your skills instead of your cash, by adding revenue, cutting cost, or removing the owner's pain.
- **Parts:** Three deal types: revenue share, profit share, straight equity; never straight equity without cash flow (S028). Equity done right is "equity plus distributions" (S035). Value matrix: estimate the owner's revenue, margin, and multiple from the outside, show what your added profit is worth, then ask for a slice (S028). Example: keep the owner whole on the first $150K, take 25% of everything above it (S020). Equity leverage teeter-totter: the less profitable and stable the business, and the more proven you are, the more you can ask for (S028). Vest sweat equity over three to five years with a one-year cliff (S015).
- **When to use:** Partnerships, agency or contractor deals, sponsor deals, key-employee comp, and "should I take equity instead of fees" questions.
- **How to score:** Prefer structures that pay cash now (distributions or revenue share) plus upside later. Pure equity in an illiquid, unprofitable company scores low.
- **Quote:** "the number one thing you can mess up is doing them just for straight Equity not cash flow" (S028)
- **Also:** "That is equity done right. Distributions today, future profits tomorrow." (S035)
- **Sources:** 15 (S003, S004, S005, S006, S012, S013, S015, S020, S023, S026, S028, S035, S036, S040, S052)

### 11. Personal P&L review and the Venmo challenge
- **What it is:** Your expenses are your deal flow. Look at who you already pay and turn a liability into an asset.
- **Parts:** Export 30 days of card, bank, Venmo, and PayPal activity; strip out big companies; list owner, access, monthly spend, their revenue, and your value add; talk to the owner; offer to grow it for a piece (S020, S026). Core satellite: your core business is the center; buy the vendors and adjacent services around it (S024). Strike Fire Productions is her proof: about $10K for a large stake in her podcast producer (S020, S049).
- **When to use:** A founder looking for growth, margin, or a first acquisition without much cash.
- **How to score:** Can you reach the owner, do you want to own it, and are you material to their revenue? Three yeses make it a target.
- **Quote:** "what if you could turn one or two of those expenses into an asset, not a liability?" (S012)
- **Also:** "Turn every expense into an income stream. I was spending too much on window cleaning, so I bought part of the window cleaning company." (S057)
- **Sources:** 13 (S006, S009, S012, S017, S020, S023, S024, S026, S029, S049, S052, S056, S057)

## Protect the downside

### 12. Never bankrupt: sizing and stress tests
- **What it is:** The first rule is survival. Size every bet so a total loss teaches rather than ruins.
- **Parts:** No deal that could lose more than 20% of net worth, now about 5% for her (S016). Model revenue down 25%, 50%, and 75%; if you cannot cover debt service, pass (S016, S042). No personal guarantee on early deals (S016). Keep 12 months of emergency cash (S016). The first deal must not be your worst, or you will never do another (S029, S052). Mosquito bites are fine; great white bites are not (S052). The CEO's one job is protecting the ability to persevere (S048).
- **When to use:** Every decision that commits cash, debt, or a guarantee.
- **How to score:** Worst-case loss as a percentage of net worth and months of runway after the worst case. Over her thresholds, the answer is no regardless of upside.
- **Quote:** "no deals that you could lose more than 20 percent of your net worth on that's my number" (S016)
- **Also:** "If the business is doing 100K right now what happens if it drops by 25k can I still pay off my monthly nut" (S042)
- **Sources:** 16 (S001, S005, S007, S008, S011, S016, S018, S019, S023, S029, S037, S042, S048, S052, S058, S064)

### 13. Debt as a tool: four kinds
- **What it is:** You don't get rich without debt, but only debt that makes you more free.
- **Parts:** Four kinds: borrowing against assets, business debt (SBA and seller financing), mortgages, credit cards (S060). Rule: if the asset grows faster than the cost of the debt, the math works. Her comfort: 20 to 30% loan to value on stocks, under 10% on volatile assets (S060). Good debt sits on assets and companies, not personal guarantees on your house (S050). First-time buyers should not exceed about 30% leverage; lenders watch debt service coverage around 1.4 to 1.6 (S058). Kill anything above about 10% interest first (S057). Explain the debt to someone smarter and richer, or don't take it (S047).
- **When to use:** Financing growth, inventory, an acquisition, or a house; any loan with a personal guarantee.
- **How to score:** Cost of debt versus asset return, coverage ratio, and what happens on a margin call or a bad quarter.
- **Quote:** "Debt should make you more free. If it makes you more fragile, you're using it wrong." (S060)
- **Also:** "Actually leverage doesn't magnify you. It magnifies all your mistakes." (S055)
- **Sources:** 11 (S002, S009, S034, S037, S047, S050, S055, S057, S058, S060, S065)

### 14. Risk buckets and "hair on a deal"
- **What it is:** Name the kind of risk, then hunt for deals with manageable risk and outsized reward.
- **Parts:** Brad Jacobs' quadrant: most of life is low risk, low reward; avoid high risk, high reward; look for "hair on a deal" where the problem looks big but the risk is manageable (S050). Three risk buckets plus one: product, market, execution, and "Ferrari risk," the shiny object (S055). Two business risks: execution risk early, asymmetric risk later (one regulator can end you) (S034). Three business killers: regulation, lawsuits, running out of cash (S023).
- **When to use:** Any bet where the founder is excited and the downside is fuzzy.
- **How to score:** List each risk, who controls it, and whether it can be capped with terms (escrow, holdbacks, milestones). Uncappable asymmetric risk is a pass.
- **Quote:** "You want to look for those hairy deals where you think, oh, that level of risk is manageable." (S050)
- **Sources:** 7 (S016, S023, S034, S048, S050, S055, S060)

### 15. Partnership rules
- **What it is:** Assume every partner will leave, and structure for it up front.
- **Parts:** No equity up front without cash in; never equity without vesting; no 50/50, someone controls; a partnership agreement with deliverables and a pre-exit ("prenup") clause (S016). No business with anyone she has not watched do business for a year (S034, S036, S040). "No New Friends": friends go through the full diligence list via someone else on her team (S017). Three vetting questions: tell me about a time everything went wrong; what is the perfect scenario in three to five years; who did you work with before, then call them (S036). After a fraud: tranched, milestone-based investing, separate banks and accountants, and daily account access (S052).
- **When to use:** Co-founders, operators with equity, joint ventures, investors, and any "my friend wants in" deal.
- **How to score:** Count the rules broken. Each one is a known failure mode she has paid for personally.
- **Quote:** "no Partners get Equity up front without cash in the game never give away Equity without a vesting schedule" (S016)
- **Also:** "I never get into business with somebody that I haven't done and watched do business for a year." (S036)
- **Sources:** 12 (S009, S015, S016, S017, S029, S030, S034, S036, S040, S041, S052, S055)

## Run it like an owner

### 16. Owner, not job (the operator threshold)
- **What it is:** If the business depends on you, you bought a job. Buy and build things big enough to pay someone else to run them.
- **Parts:** The income stream must cover four things: debt service, your income, an operator's salary, and working capital (S024, S035). Around $100K of profit is where you can hire someone good (S006). "Find the human before you find the business" if you don't want to run it (S015). You are still self-employed if fulfillment, sales, or distribution falls entirely on you (S062). Give operators equity (often 30 to 40%) with vesting (S023).
- **When to use:** Any plan where the founder is the only seller, the only creative, or the only face.
- **How to score:** Remove the founder for 90 days on paper. If revenue or delivery stops, it is a job, and it is worth far less at sale.
- **Quote:** "If you are the only salesperson you don't have a business you have a job and if you only have one salesperson you don't have a sales team you have a liability" (S022)
- **Also:** "the more that you are the driver of your revenue, the less you have a business, the more you have a highly paid job." (S062)
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S006, S008, S011, S015, S022, S023, S024, S035, S036, S040, S051, S062, S065)

### 17. Cash suck versus cash flow
- **What it is:** Get paid before you deliver. A business that floats its customers is fragile and worth less.
- **Parts:** Cash suck: serve first, invoice later, lumpy, one big client. Cash flow: paid up front, recurring, many clients (S022). Run a 13-week cash flow and check the real bank balance twice a week (S040, S065). Cash crunch levers: raise prices, make customers pay sooner, extend your own terms, ask the bank to extend, negotiate rent; spend 10% on the problem, 90% on solutions (S036). In COVID she called landlords and lenders: "some money is better than no money" (S066).
- **When to use:** Any growth plan, new client contract, or payment terms decision.
- **How to score:** Days between spending and collecting. Shorter is safer; negative (paid before you spend) is best.
- **Quote:** "Most small business owners can't afford more than 30 days of no cash flow." (S048)
- **Also:** "Most businesses die because the owner gives up or because you run out of cash." (S065)
- **Sources:** 9 (S021, S022, S031, S036, S040, S041, S048, S065, S066)

### 18. The 12 profit levers and the pricing ladder *(Own or Be Owned)*
- **What it is:** Her 2026 operating system for owners: score the business on 12 levers against a data set of about 30,000 businesses, then work them in 90-day loops.
- **Parts:** First three to check: price too low, selling to the wrong persona, no process for profits (S065). Most small businesses are underpriced 30% to 300%; of about 1,600 analyzed, none were overpriced (S065). Pricing ladder: market rate, then 10 to 20% above competitors, then value-based at 10 to 30% of value delivered (S065). Three prices for everything plus a recurring option (S041). Owner archetypes affect pricing: artists and founders price 30 to 40% lower than closers (S064).
- **When to use:** Margin pressure, a "we need more leads" complaint, or an owner who has not raised prices in years.
- **How to score:** Last price increase date, price versus competitors, share of revenue from best-fit customers. Leads complaints often turn out to be product, profit, or expense problems (S037).
- **Quote:** "For most small businesses, they're underpriced 30-300%. In those 1,600 businesses we've analyzed, you want to know how many were overpriced? Zero." (S065)
- **Also:** "I don't think you have a pricing problem. I think you have a confidence problem." (S062)
- **Sources:** 12 (S020, S024, S025, S037, S041, S053, S059, S062, S063, S064, S065, S066)

### 19. Two-oar metrics and Financial Fridays
- **What it is:** Run on two numbers per team, reviewed weekly, so the owner can oversee without operating.
- **Parts:** Two metrics, like paddling a canoe: one revenue measure and one top-of-funnel measure; one metric and you go in circles (S052, S062). Financial Fridays (later Tuesdays): weekly P&L and dashboard review (S004, S041). Rule of three: more than three steps, done more than three times, gets an SOP (S022). Green and yellow flags; she only digs into yellow (S022). PE "top five" systems: CEO dashboard, monthly P&L, 90-day sprints, 13-week cash flow, mission and vision dashboard (S065).
- **When to use:** A founder who wants out of the weeds, or an investor deciding whether a business can run without its owner.
- **How to score:** Can a buyer understand the scorecard in 60 seconds (S022)? If not, the business is not ready for oversight or sale.
- **Quote:** "If it has more than three steps and you do it more than three times I made a standard operating procedure for it" (S022)
- **Sources:** 9 (S004, S011, S022, S040, S041, S042, S052, S062, S065)

### 20. Lifestyle, scale, or exit (pick one per year)
- **What it is:** Every business is run for lifestyle, scale, or exit. Owners who want all three get none.
- **Parts:** Choose one each year and tell the whole team (S065). Contrarian Thinking is a scale business in 2026, so most money goes back in (S064, S065). Matches her "start with the end in mind" goals matrix: money, timeline, team, transition, legacy (S022).
- **When to use:** Strategy offsites, hiring plans, and any decision that trades profit now for growth later.
- **How to score:** Does the option serve this year's declared mode? If it serves a different mode, it needs an explicit reason.
- **Quote:** "We believe you should pick one per year, and the whole team should know." (S065)
- **Sources:** 4 (S022, S040, S064, S065)

### 21. Horizontal income and income stacking
- **What it is:** Passive income is a myth; horizontal income is several streams, each needing only oversight. Inside one business, sell more to the customers you already have.
- **Parts:** Laundromat case: buy at $67K profit, add vending, buy the competitor, buy struggling rivals' machines, add delivery wash and fold, turn soap into a product, buy the real estate, with zero new customers needed for the first steps (S048). Seven acquisition types inside one business (S036, S041). Concentration rules: no client over 15% of revenue, no product over 60%, multiple payment processors and platforms (S022). Be "hard to kill": a regulator killed four of her businesses (S048).
- **When to use:** Revenue concentration, platform dependence, or a founder seeking "passive" income.
- **How to score:** Largest client share, largest product share, number of platforms you depend on. Any single point of failure is a red flag.
- **Quote:** "Make sure that no one client makes up more than 15% of your Revenue" (S022)
- **Also:** "I want your business to become really hard to kill." (S048)
- **Sources:** 12 (S004, S013, S015, S021, S022, S025, S030, S035, S036, S041, S048, S051)

## Sell it, and build personal wealth

### 22. The Cash Out Cake (build to sell)
- **What it is:** Start with the end in mind; a sellable business is profitable, documented, owner-independent, de-risked, and recurring.
- **Parts:** Layers: trusted profitability and tax returns; documented systems; a management layer instead of key-person risk; diversified customers and recurring revenue; projections that show a brighter future (S022, S037, S040). Recurring revenue types: contracts, subscriptions, replenishment, memberships (S022). Small businesses sell for roughly 2 to 3x profit, sometimes 3 to 5x; strategic buyers pay more (S022, S035). Give yourself 12 to 18 months to double or triple the sale price (S022).
- **When to use:** Any decision that affects valuation: naming the business after the founder, taking a giant client, custom work.
- **How to score:** Would a buyer pay more or less for the business after this decision?
- **Quote:** "You can't sell a job but you can sell an asset." (S040)
- **Also:** "Don't name your business your name you want brand loyalty not personal loyalty" (S022)
- **Sources:** 8 (S011, S022, S035, S036, S037, S040, S041, S065)

### 23. Learn, earn, invest: the wealth ladder
- **What it is:** You can't skip rungs. Learn, then raise earnings, then invest, then own.
- **Parts:** Learn, earn, invest (S013, S014, S023). Four levels of rich: spender, saver, owner, investor (S025). Investing stages: yourself, low-cost index funds, private assets, then become the company others invest in (S050). Wealth Elevator by income, from tracking every dollar to acquiring whole businesses (S057). The first $100K usually comes from wages; what gets you to $1M won't get you to $10M (S021, S030).
- **When to use:** Personal money decisions, quitting a job, or a founder overreaching for their stage.
- **How to score:** Which rung is the person on, and does the option skip one? Skipping rungs is where she sees losses.
- **Quote:** "The thing that got you to 100K will probably get you to a million bucks but it won't get you to 10 million." (S030)
- **Sources:** 10 (S011, S013, S014, S021, S023, S025, S030, S050, S056, S057)

### 24. Recession playbook ("a sale is coming")
- **What it is:** Downturns transfer assets from the unprepared to the prepared. Keep cash and credit ready, then buy.
- **Parts:** Hold reserves (she disagrees with zero-cash advice, S023); buy recession-resistant businesses, raise prices, add technology (S025); circle up partners for one smart purchase; big institutions also face a credit crunch, which is the small buyer's window (S025). "Now is the time to learn, not always to allocate" when private valuations lag public ones (S008).
- **When to use:** Market downturns, rate spikes, distressed sellers, or a founder tempted to spend reserves.
- **How to score:** Cash and credit available, valuation versus last year, and whether the target still clears the downside test.
