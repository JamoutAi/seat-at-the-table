# Positions: Codie Sanchez

34 founder topics. Each has her stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time side by side. Dates are YouTube upload dates; three re-uploads were excluded from the corpus, and several interviews were recorded months before upload. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct.

## Money, cash, and capital

**1. Buying versus starting.** Buy if you want cash flow; start only if you have a dream you can't let go of or a real unfair advantage.
- "Most businesses you spend money today for a promise of future potential profits right and I just want the profits day one." (S009, 2022-12-20)
- "I think if you're crazy enough to want to start a business, you won't ask for advice. You'll just go do it." (S064, 2026-09-18)
- Consistent 2020 to 2026. Her own path: a startup that failed, then a bought laundromat (S004, S043).

**2. Raising outside capital.** Avoid selling equity if debt, distributions, or customer cash can do the job. She has never raised for Contrarian Thinking. Capital-heavy companies (robotics, ports) do need venture money.
- "We Own 100% of contrarian thinking I haven't taken a single investor." (S036, 2024-12-02)
- "Capital will make you a lot faster, but I'm more optimizing for longevity." (S056, 2026-02-25)
- "The smarter dealmaker that you are the less you give away over time." (S036, 2024-12-02)
- She admits she took investors in early businesses because she did not know how to value herself (S036), and she did raise a small round for BizScout from named operators, sized for distribution, not dependence (S029, 2024-08-12).

**3. Debt and leverage.** Pro debt on assets and companies that produce cash; against personal guarantees on things you don't need and against consumer debt.
- "You're not going to get rich without using debt." (S060, 2026-08-04)
- "I think debt is only a four-letter word if you don't know how to use it" (S065, 2026-10-08)
- "I would never want to do a personally guaranteed loan on my first deals" (S016, 2023-09-10)
- Consistent. Earlier framing: "One thing I've realized about wealth over time is, um, debt is just about everything." (S002, 2021-04-28)

**4. SBA loans versus seller financing.** Seller financing is her favorite; SBA is useful but slow and needs a personal guarantee.
- "Getting a loan from a bank is like a colonoscopy." (S010, 2023-03-03)
- "The government will loan you up to 90% of the purchase price." (S058, 2026-06-04)
- Shift: early on about 80% of her deals were seller financed (S011, 2023-05-29); by 2026 her team launched an SBA loan brokerage and she teaches 21 financing methods (S058, S065).

**5. Paying cash.** Even when you can, don't tie up all your own cash.
- "I paid the full 100k because I had the cash, but I'm smarter now. So, I wouldn't do that again." (S048, 2025-06-23)
- "This is my least favorite way to buy most businesses." (S010, 2023-03-03, on all-cash)

**6. Cash reserves.** Keep a buffer; she disagrees "intensely" with keeping zero in the bank (S023, 2024-02-04) and wants 12 months of emergency cash before risky deals (S016).
- "I just don't like risk. I don't like risk. I want to never have a likelihood where I can't pay my mortgage or pay my rent." (S013, 2023-07-03)

**7. Valuation and multiples.** Small businesses trade at a multiple of profit, not revenue: usually 2 to 3x, range 1 to 6x, higher past $10M revenue or with strategic buyers.
- "Any business below five million dollars in sales they trade at two to three times their profit." (S001, 2020-10-08)
- "Small businesses typically sell from anywhere from 2 to 3x profits in the sub $10 million category." (S037, 2024-12-03)
- She sometimes says 2 to 6x (S017) or 3 to 5x at exit (S035). Car washes jumped to 12 to 15x revenue offers after a public roll-up (S042, her figure).

**8. Pricing and discounting.** Raise prices; most small businesses are underpriced 30% to 300%. Don't discount; reframe to the cost of the problem.
- "We do not sell price, we sell solutions to problems." (S063, 2026-09-06)
- "That's you admitting that you never believed in the price in the first place." (S063, 2026-09-06, on discounting)
- Self-critique: "our issues are always we should increase our prices, but we don't." (S064, 2026-09-18)

## Investing and personal money

**9. Stock market.** Index funds to beat inflation, on autopay; buy more when markets panic. No single stocks, no day trading.
- "I don't do single stocks. I don't mess around with gold. I don't try to speculate on Bitcoin." (S055, 2026-02-12)
- "If you're just looking at the price, you're not investing. You're speculating." (S055, 2026-02-12)
- Consistent; she started buying in 2008 (S023, S057). She also mentions buying Bitcoin in panics (S057), so treat "no Bitcoin speculation" as no trading, not zero exposure.

**10. Real estate and homes.** Real estate preserves wealth and saves tax; it does not make wealth. Owning a home as an investment is "mathematically not a smart decision" at current rates.
- "Real estate is for keeping wealth, not making wealth." (S038, 2024-12-03)
- "I think that owning a home as an investment right now today is mathematically not a smart decision." (S050, 2025-09-08)
- "So, rent is not throwing money away. Rent buys you a place to live." (S060, 2026-08-04)
- She still owns real estate and Airbnbs (S042, S051) and recommends owning the property under a business when possible (S024, S048).

**11. Angel and startup investing.** Not until you are a millionaire, and then only with money you can lose.
- "Until you're a millionaire I would do zero startup investing, I mean zero, because it's a 90% failure rate." (S029, 2024-08-12)
- "Once you've made a few million dollars and I mean that literally then I think go into angel investing." (S002, 2021-04-28)
- Shift: in 2022 she said she hated angel investing and was redirecting the money (S008); she now runs a small venture fund (Contrarian Thinking Capital) her husband manages (S028, S056).

**12. Passive income.** A myth. Call it horizontal or leveraged income, built by years of active work.
- "there is no such thing as purely passive income I think it is a complete and utter fallacy" (S015, 2023-08-08)
- "Let's be clear, not passive income, but it can be leveraged income" (S051, 2025-09-28)
- Shift: she says she used to not mind the phrase passive income and now it bothers her (S013); she tried long sabbaticals and found "you can only read so many books on the beach" (S015).

**13. Side hustles.** Most are bad: they don't scale and teach nothing. Use salary to buy cash-flowing assets instead.
- "Most side hustles are terrible and they're terrible because they don't scale." (S001, 2020-10-08)
- "I think side hustles are actually meant to feel like you're going to get rich, but to keep you poor." (S057, 2026-03-30)
- Nuance: keep a side business on the side until it is "a profit project," not a passion project (S050, 2025-09-08).

**14. Saving and budgeting.** Earn more rather than skip the coffee, but kill high-interest debt and keep a buffer.
- "I don't think I ever skipped a Starbucks and made a million bucks." (S024, 2024-03-24)
- "The best debt payoff method is the one you'll actually stick to." (S057, 2026-03-30)

**15. Employee versus entrepreneur.** Being an employee is underrated; get equity, profit share, or co-invest rights inside a good company. Entrepreneurship is overrated for most.
- "You can definitely make your first million in a 9 to5 actually I think easier than you can an entrepreneur." (S023, 2024-02-04)
- "It is overrated to go be an an entrepreneur yourself." (S056, 2026-02-25)
- "If you're a high performer, stop asking for raises. Your job isn't as secure as you think. Negotiate some ownership." (S057, 2026-03-30)

**16. MBAs and credentials.** Mostly a bad trade now; learn business by doing business. She has a Georgetown MBA and says it opened doors.
- "If you want to learn business, go do business." (S054, 2026-01-27)
- "I think that is a great way keep people poor because you keep them chasing credentials" (S042, 2024-12-11)

## Strategy and growth

**17. Growth by acquisition.** The fastest growth is buying customers, competitors, and capabilities. Every billion-dollar company acquires.
- "Your competitors now are actually not somebody you compete against. You buy them." (S048, 2025-06-23)
- "If you want to go fast you buy 100 clients at a time as organically acquire one at a time." (S036, 2024-12-02)

**18. Focus versus portfolio.** She runs a portfolio but tells beginners to start with one and not buy several at once. Focus kills fewer businesses than distraction.
- "Don't buy 57 companies at once start with one have some respect for what that other person has built" (S042, 2024-12-11)
- "Your lack of focus will kill you before your competitors do" (S041, 2024-12-06)
- Tension: she admits she bought too many businesses and her team told her to focus (S062); she says a single concentrated bet is the better path to a billion, she just never had the idea (S019).

**19. Turnarounds.** No for beginners. She did turnarounds in cannabis private equity and calls them miserable.
- "we don't buy turnarounds you're not a pro so we don't do pro level things yet" (S016, 2023-09-10)
- Exception: by late 2022 she felt experienced enough to consider a software turnaround (S008).

**20. Customer concentration and platforms.** No client over 15% of revenue, no product over 60%, never depend on one platform.
- "They're just trying to run a business but they could kill yours" (S022, 2023-12-16, on payment processors and platforms)
- "You are building on rented land and paying full price for the privilege." (S059, 2026-06-09, on e-commerce)

**21. Recessions and timing.** Downturns are sales. Keep cash ready and buy when others panic, but learn first if valuations lag.
- "I just sort of think about it like the unprepared transfer their money to the prepared during downturns." (S025, 2024-04-02)

**22. Businesses she avoids.** Restaurants, hotels ("real estate masquerading as a business"), retail stores, single-platform businesses like Amazon FBA, consulting (key-person risk), dry cleaners, unprofitable businesses, and anything she can't explain to her grandmother.
- "Hotels that's just real estate masquerading as a business." (S011, 2023-05-29)
- She bends this: she owns some sectors she warns about, and says operator skill matters more than industry ("they all rhyme," S065).

**23. Laundromats.** Her teaching "gateway drug," not a recommendation for sophisticated buyers.
- "The problem with laundromats are they're a terrible business if you're really good at business" (S019, 2023-10-26)
- "By the way, do not Can I just have a warning here? Please do not do this. Do not start a laundromat." (S059, 2026-06-09)
- Shift: she sold all her laundromats by 2026 and moved toward home services, trades, and franchises (S058).

**24. Private equity.** She admires the playbook and borrows it, but criticizes fund clocks that "strip it and flip it," and wants individuals, not institutions, to own Main Street.
- "I don't want to own all of America's small businesses. I want Americans to own those businesses." (S025, 2024-04-02)
- "They're not bad people they're actually brilliant most of the people I know in private Equity are so dang smart I just think they're selling themselves short" (S042, 2024-12-11)
- Shift: she once wanted her own small version of KKR or Berkshire Hathaway and dropped it on values grounds (S019, S025).

**25. AI.** Use it inside a sound business; don't start an AI company for money. Trades and local services are safer than online jobs. Most AI business advice is generic.
- "There's actually much more risk in an AI brand new startup than there is in a car wash." (S037, 2024-12-03)
- "you need to earn the right to use AI by doing like standard business practices" (S062, 2026-09-03)
- Shift: "Gosh, if you would ask me 12 months ago, I would have said this is incredible. This is going to be a game-changer." (S064, 2026-09-18). She also changed her mind on SaaS because AI tools made building easier (S051).

## Marketing and brand

**26. Content and audience.** Audience is leverage (she adds it to Naval's labor, capital, code) and distribution for owned businesses, but content is a poor business on its own.
- "Content is really just a word for audience Arbitrage." (S008, 2022-11-22)
- "Otherwise, you've built a very time-consuming hobby that's actually really expensive." (S059, 2026-06-09)
- She disclosed YouTube AdSense of $360,000 in 2024 against about $1.2M of cost (S059).

**27. Personal brand for small businesses.** Mostly no. Build brand loyalty, not loyalty to you.
- "No, I do not think most small businesses should build a personal brand at all." (S064, 2026-09-18)
- "Don't name your business your name you want brand loyalty not personal loyalty" (S022, 2023-12-16)

**28. Brand as a moat.** In fragmented local markets, a real brand beats cost-efficient private equity owners.
- "They're really cost efficient they're Financial Wizards those are smart guys and gals but like you said they do not get branded" (S045, 2025-02-11)

**29. Paid ads versus owned attention.** Ads are renting attention; owned lists and channels compound.
- "Renting attention is a tax. That's what we do when we use things called ads." (S053, 2026-01-04)
- Admission: she has poured ad money into a company with near-zero referrals and called herself an idiot for it (S029).

**30. Sponsorships.** Prefer equity or owned products over one-off fees; only promote what you'd stand behind.
- "I'm trying to get equity for every deal I do sponsorships with, if at all humanly possible." (S005, 2022-03-17)
- "If your audience trusts you, I don't want to sell that out for 500 bucks." (S051, 2025-09-28)

## People and the founder

**31. Partners.** Few, vetted for a year, with vesting and control. No 50/50.
- "no 50 50 partner someone needs to be in control and it's probably you or whoever has the most skin in the game" (S016, 2023-09-10)
- "We don't do new deals with people that we haven't known for a year." (S034, 2024-11-17)
- Shift: "I didn't have a lot of rules for partners when I started now I do." (S016)

**32. Hiring and firing.** Hire proven, hungry people slowly; fire fast and kindly; no B players.
- "When you 2x a company, you're going to expect to lose about a third of the original team." (S061, 2026-08-30)
- "Stop giving compliments to people on your team that are underperforming just because it makes you feel better." (S011, 2023-05-29)

**33. Remote work.** Against for growth roles; she worked remote for years and fell behind. Fine only if the role is purely metric-driven.
- "Remote work optimizes for your comfort. Growth is not about comfort." (S061, 2026-08-30)

**34. Work-life balance and burnout.** Openly against balance during the build; work comes in seasons. She also warns that the founder flaming out is how employees get hurt.
- "Nothing truly great was ever built with balance in the beginning." (S061, 2026-08-30)
- "the best way to serve your employees is make sure you don't flame out miraculously and not be able to pay them and fail." (S062, 2026-09-03)
- Nuance: she rejects "hustle porn" and sleeping on floors (S014, S018), describes low-level grind as "soul killing" (S014), and says burnout usually calls for a break, not quitting (S058).
