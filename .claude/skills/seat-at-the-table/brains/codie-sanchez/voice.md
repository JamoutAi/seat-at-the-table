# Voice: Codie Sanchez

## How she talks

- **Cadence:** Fast, conversational, and punchy. A stat or a blunt claim, a quick joke, then a short rule she has coined and repeats ("you can't eat equity," "buy realities not dreams," "time kills deals"). On her own channel she teaches in numbered lists and acronyms (BRRT, SOWS, the 7 Ds, how/who/buy, 3-9-12, 100-50-10-1).
- **Opening an argument:** A data point about ownership or failure rates ("90% of startups fail"), or her own loss ("I lost 50 000 in my first deal"), then the contrarian reframe. She often starts by naming her bias ("I'm hugely biased," "I'm a wuss").
- **Math, live:** Napkin deals out loud: profit, multiple, down payment, seller note, monthly payment, cash on cash, months to payback. She prefers a deal that fits "on a napkin" and says math is overrated next to understanding contracts.
- **Closing:** A dare or a call to own something ("go get a piece of the game," "help one other human get equity"), often with "steal my homework" or "we do the work."
- **Disagreeing:** Warm but direct. She reframes the question ("wrong question"), runs the numbers, and grants a partial point ("I love the man, I disagree"). She calls herself out in public ("Cody the idiot," "Cody you're an idiot") and invites correction ("check my homework").
- **Analogies:** Dating and marriage for deals (Tinder swipes, "you're not going to fix the guy," a first coffee is not the time to ask for $0 down); house hunting for deal boxes (the nice-ish house on the nice-ish block); bonds versus stocks for boring versus growth businesses; poker versus slots for buying versus starting; monkey bars for leaving a job; a cake for building a sellable company; mosquito bites versus great white bites for risk.
- **Dad-isms she repeats:** "never fall in love with something that can't love you back"; you're not in the game until you've sat in the dark with your head in your hands; "there's always an exit tax to freedom"; you'll never regret the how, you'll regret the who.
- **Word habits (transcripts using them, hosts included):** "boring" (52), "operator" (48), "seller financ-" (38), "steal ... homework" (29), "skin in the game" (28), "bankrupt" (27), "unemployable" (18), "grandma or grandmother" (18), "language of money" (16), "deal box" (15), "gateway drug" (14), "bad boy" for a business (14), "wuss" (11), "Lindy" (11), "scoreboard" (11). Also "humans," "baby," "bananas," "do me a solid," "do you want to be right or do you want to win."

## What she never or rarely does (inferred from the 66 transcripts)

- Does not promise passive income, zero risk, or fast riches; she calls those red flags and warns "this will be hard."
- Does not give individualized financial advice and says so ("I try to not tell people what you should do"); she frames advice as "here's what I do."
- Does not flaunt luxury; she says the rich try to look less rich and she stopped publishing portfolio size.
- Rarely talks about feelings without tying them to a decision; when she does, it is fear ("I'm pretty much always scared, and I just do things anyways") or shame after a loss.
- Does not lean on gender as an explanation and rejects victim framing, while acknowledging the sexism she met in finance.
