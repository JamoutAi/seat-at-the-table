# Positions: Jamie Kern Lima

30 founder topics. Each has her stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time where they exist. Dates are YouTube upload dates. Many uploads on her own channel (2023 to 2026) are compilations of interviews recorded earlier, mostly around the *Believe IT* launch (2021) and the *Worthy* launch (2024), so treat dates as approximate. Undated sources show "n.d." Her figures are as she tells them and drift between tellings (website orders "1 to 2" or "2 to 3" a day; QVC airings in year two "101" or "150"; when the investor wrote back, "six" or "eight" years later).

## Product and customers

**1. Product development.** Make something that truly works for a real problem, iterate until it does, then build the mission on top. She says the first concealer took hundreds of iterations and the product alone was not enough.
- "We poured all of our money into R&D into making a product and um and then I had no money to hire anybody." (S020, n.d.)
- Her starting mistake: "if the product's great and our mission is so great, it's just going to work. It's just going to sell." (S025, n.d.) Consistent 2019 to 2026.

**2. Who the customer is.** A specific woman, pictured concretely, who has never seen herself in the category's marketing. Not the founder's niche: most IT customers had no skin issues.
- "I know who my customer is and i imagine that woman on the other end of the tv." (S042, 2021-04-27)
- "I think it's like 95 the last time I checked of our customers have no skin issues." (S050, 2021-05-10)

**3. Do you need to solve your own problem?** No. Changed emphasis: her early story is "if I have this problem, others do," but by 2021 she says the scratch-your-own-itch story puts too much pressure on people; what is required is a deeply personal why.
- "I don't think that your company or your business has to be solving your own problem." (S013, 2025-01-15)
- "I do think that it has to be attached to a why that is so deeply personal to you" (S027, 2021-02-24)

**4. Saying no to trends and buyer requests.** Say no when it does not serve the core customer or fit the brand DNA, even if a retailer asks and the team wants the sales.
- "I got really good at disappointing people when I knew it wasn't aligned with our authentic mission of the brand." (S003, n.d.)
- "I had to get good at disappointing the retail stores." (S033, 2024-04-22)
- Consistent.

**5. Packaging and price positioning.** Kept simple, usable packaging because fancy packaging sits on the counter unused, and kept a prestige price with real models although retailers said you could not do both.
- "I would put it on my counter and I would look at it but I wouldn't use it." (S018, 2023-10-18)
- "I thought you can't use real women and still charge a high price point." (S010, n.d.) Here she is voicing what retailers told her.

**6. First mover advantage.** Skeptical. Her early contour palettes flopped and were later copied by bigger brands that won on timing.
- "Some of the much bigger brands at the time um actually knocked off our products exactly like the exact colors, the exact pallets, the exact design." (S020, n.d.)

**7. Copycats and knockoffs.** A giant competitor paid off her manufacturer for her formula; the copy "just did okay" because it did not fit the copier's positioning. Lesson: protect IP early, and you cannot copy authenticity.
- "This was a product that even though it was a great one was in congruent with their positioning." (S021, n.d.)
- "Protect your IP early on and get your formula ownership early on before the manufacturers care about you." (S014, n.d.)

## Marketing and sales

**8. Real-people marketing.** The core of her brand: real models of every age, size, skin tone, and skin challenge, and the founder bare-faced on live TV. She predicted others would copy it if it worked.
- "If i could be successful doing that i was like i bet you other brands will copy me." (S012, 2021-07-30)
- "I'm creating a company that authentically believes every person is beautiful so why wouldn't I show every person as the models." (S018, 2023-10-18)

**9. Founder as the face of the brand.** Powerful early, dangerous later. Fine to use someone else as the face if it is authentic; fatal if the business depends on one person.
- "We had a business for a number for for a few years that was key person dependent on me going on air all the time." (S016, 2025-01-08)

**10. Live selling and TV shopping.** QVC let her prove the product live and served as her advertising when she had no budget. Selling must be about the viewer, not the seller.
- "You can sell more product in an hour on QVC than in a whole year in department stores." (S050, 2021-05-10)
- "I guess you is probably the most common word I would use because I knew it wasn't about me" (S027, 2021-02-24)

**11. Copying what works for others online.** Against it. Comparing and copying dilutes your secret sauce and blocks connection with your own customers.
- "You are robbing yourself of of that actual authentic connection with your own customers your own Community." (S018, 2023-10-18)
- "The temptation of of diluting your own authenticity to do that to me is the worst thing an entrepreneur can ever do." (S010, n.d.)

**12. Selling as service.** Reframe outreach as a gift to the client.
- "Pick up the phone and just call 50 new potential clients and drown out my own self-doubt and know that it's a gift to them if we work together." (S009, 2021-02-25)

**13. Social media and the direct-to-consumer era.** Mixed. No barriers to entry is good, but markets are flooded and many founders look bigger online than they are.
- "So many people right now are Overexposed but underdeveloped and I think that that is um a big risk." (S021, n.d.)
- "It's so tempting to spend money on things that look really good online but don't actually serve your customers." (S020, n.d.)

## Money, funding, and risk

**14. Bootstrapping.** The default. Three-plus years without pay, under $1,000 in a combined account, nothing glamorous until cash flow was safe.
- "We got down to under a thousand dollars in our bank account which was our company and personal combined." (S002, 2021-03-01)
- "We did everything possible to stay cash flow positive." (S010, n.d.)

**15. Outside investors and private equity.** Changed with stage. Early and desperate: a no protected her equity. Later and stronger: she took private equity from TSG and liked it.
- "We probably would have sold him the majority of the company for like no money because he didn't believe in us." (S002, 2021-03-01)
- "We did take on private equity a few years later. Uh and that was an amazing experience like amazing experience." (S020, n.d.)
- "We did have investors by that time but we had stayed the largest shareholder all the way through the sale." (S009, 2021-02-25)

**16. Bank debt.** Took an SBA loan with a personal guarantee to fund one order, and concedes the banks that refused were right to.
- "We went to 22 banks trying to get a loan, a SBA loan. They all said no. And they should have because we had no accounts receivable." (S025, n.d.)

**17. Big risky orders.** Teaches the rule against it, broke it once with eyes open.
- "You should never take a purchase order you can't afford to lose, and we definitely couldn't afford to lose it." (S053, 2021-08-19)

**18. Spending on image.** Against it at every stage, including as an investor in over 15 companies.
- "They're spending their money in ways that don't add value to their customers or to their mission but maybe fuel their ego." (S033, 2024-04-22)

**19. Selling the company versus going public.** Chose a 100% sale to L'Oreal over an IPO: more money possible from going public, but she wanted a partner with teams in 100+ countries and did not trust herself to stop overworking.
- "We had the opportunity to go public and probably would have made way more money." (S042, 2021-04-27)
- "I had to trust myself not to trust myself because i was so addicted to work at that point." (S042, 2021-04-27)
- "We can figure this out on our own, but it's going to take a we're going to go a whole lot slower." (S014, n.d.)

**20. Negotiating an exit.** Create competitive tension and be willing to say no to the first offer.
- "I didn't even know I knew how to say the word no. I only knew how to hear the word no." (S014, n.d.)

## Team, leadership, and operations

**21. Hiring.** Hire for mission fit and authentic belief over polished credentials; the mission recruits people who feel unseen elsewhere.
- "I took three people internally and I taught those people who had the authenticity piece I taught them the TV and the sales piece." (S016, 2025-01-08)

**22. Firing.** Admits she fired too slowly for years and links it to her adoption and fear of abandoning people.
- "One of my biggest weaknesses as a boss is I would not fire people soon enough because I didn't want to abandon them." (S040, 2024-10-29)
- "I'm going to love that person from a new healthy boundary and a big old distance and it's not my job to rescue them." (S040, 2024-10-29)

**23. Scaling never gets easier.** More people means a new job: managing them.
- "Even if you hire the best of the best people, you still then have a new job, which is which is managing people." (S014, n.d.)

**24. Leading by consensus.** Against it for big calls; for novel ideas leaders must trust themselves.
- "When we lead by consensus, it's often not the most effective decision." (S035, 2022-03-02)
- "True leaders have novel ideas. And most leaders are scared to act on them." (S035, 2022-03-02)

**25. Diversity in leadership.** Teams and boardrooms should look like the customer.
- "I believe that not just our teams but our boardrooms should look like our customers do" (S023, 2025-03-19)

**26. Co-founding with a spouse.** Possible but brutal; boundaries failed for a decade.
- "It's one of the hardest things i've ever done in my life i it is a miracle that my husband and i are still married." (S012, 2021-07-30)
- "We were never able to set boundaries between work and personal." (S012, 2021-07-30)

## The founder

**27. Work hours and burnout.** Clear change of view. During the build she did 100-hour weeks for about a decade; since 2020 she says that was a mistake driven by fear and low self-worth.
- 2019: "I never figured out how to have any semblance of balance." (S008, 2019-12-18)
- 2021 onward: "I don't believe you have to work 100 hour weeks to build the kind of business that i did." (S012, 2021-07-30)
- "I was just driving it so hard because i had a fear it could all come crashing down" (S026, 2021-02-23)

**28. Celebrating success and visibility.** Changed view. She hid the Forbes list for about three years, then decided hiding robs other women of an example.
- "I literally hid the forbes list for almost three years." (S034, 2021-05-25)
- "If I'm dimming my own light and and I'm actually just robbing other girls, other women of another example in their life" (S024, n.d.)

**29. Rejection, still.** Inconsistent across interviews. Some say she is fearless and it no longer hurts; others say it still stings. Treat the business rule (do not take it personally) as stable, the feeling as variable.
- "I am Fearless when it comes to rejection or failure like it does not phase me at all." (S021, n.d.)
- "To this day i get rejected all the time for stuff all the time i have this idea or that idea." (S009, 2021-02-25)

**30. Self-worth and business results.** Since about 2023 her central teaching: business ceilings and sabotage are often worth problems, and building worth increases ambition. She says she only had strong self-worth in the last two or three years.
- "If you want to double your business double your selfworth." (S032, n.d.)
- "I'm 46, I've only had strong self-worth now for probably the last two years of my life, that's it." (S058, 2024-03-20)
