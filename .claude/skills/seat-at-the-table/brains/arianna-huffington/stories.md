# Stories: Arianna Huffington

18 stories and case studies she reuses, most-told first. Figures are as she tells them; several drift between tellings (stitches, rejection counts, country counts), and those ranges are noted.

1. **The collapse, April 6, 2007.** Two years into HuffPost, divorced, touring colleges with her daughter by day and working at night on four to five hours of sleep, she fainted at her desk, broke her cheekbone, and woke "in a pool of blood" (four stitches over her right eye; five in the 2010 TED version). Doctors ran MRIs and heart tests and found nothing medically wrong; she was diagnosed with burnout. "There wasn't anything wrong with me medically but just about everything wrong with the way I was leading my life." (S009) She now calls it "a blessing in a heavy disguise" (S060) and says HuffPost's biggest successes came after it. Lesson: the cost of depletion is real even when it is invisible, and success at the cost of health is not success. (S002, S004, S006, S008, S009, S012, S017, S020, S024, S035, S040, S043, S045, S046, S049, S053, S059, S062, S063, S064, S065)

2. **Mother, the one-room apartment, and Cambridge.** Raised in Athens by a mother with little money and no formal education, she saw a photo of Cambridge in a magazine. Everyone said it was impossible; her mother said "let's see how you can get there," borrowed money, and took her to look at it. Her mother made failure feel safe: "if I failed along the way she wouldn't love me any less." She became the third woman president of the Cambridge Union despite an accent people mocked. Lesson: risk tolerance is learned from knowing failure will not cost you love; "Failure is not the opposite of success it's a stepping stone to success" (S028). (S001, S017, S028, S035, S036, S041, S047, S053, S064)

3. **Rejected by 36 or 37 publishers, and the Barclays overdraft.** Her second book, on political leadership, was rejected dozens of times (36 in S011 and S037, 37 in S047 and S057; 27 then 13 more in S033). Out of money in London, thinking her first book was a fluke, she walked into Barclays on St. James's Street with "a lot of Greek chutzpah" and asked manager Ian Bell for an overdraft with no assets. He said yes, and she still sends him a holiday card. She now calls that book "so academic in a bad way and so i don't blame them" (S047). Lesson: perseverance and "helpful animals" at pivotal moments; you give up before you get there more often than you fail. (S011, S033, S037, S047, S057, S059, S064, S066)

4. **"Everything happened because a man wouldn't marry me."** For seven years from age 23 she was with the writer Bernard Levin, "twice my age and half my size," who wanted cats, not children. At 30 she moved to New York to put an ocean between them. Her daughters, her US career, HuffPost, and Thrive all followed. Lesson: setbacks open doors; life is "a dance between making it happen and letting it happen" (S018). (S005, S006, S015, S028, S041, S042, S053, S064)

5. **Launching HuffPost at 55.** After her 2003 governor run taught her "the power of digital news," she launched HuffPost in May 2005 with Ken Lerer, about $1M to $2M from friends, and a day-one roster of famous bloggers (Larry David, Nora Ephron, Walter Cronkite) gathered by emailing everyone she knew. She paid her teenage daughters to moderate comments. Lesson: elevate a dismissed format (blogging), make it easy for busy people to contribute, and "the founder game is not just a young person's game" (S035). (S001, S017, S028, S035, S047, S062, S064)

6. **"An unsurvivable failure."** A day-one review called HuffPost "an unsurvivable failure," the movie equivalent of Ishtar and Heaven's Gate. A year later the critic wrote to say they were wrong and asked to blog for her, and she said yes. Lesson: do not let naysayers define you and do not hold grudges. (S005, S030, S035, S053, S057, S064)

7. **The sleep section her board hated.** In 2007, after her collapse, she launched a dedicated sleep section at HuffPost "when people were laughing at us" (S026), and her board was "appalled" (S049). By 2016, wellness topics drove about half of HuffPost's traffic and, by her account, about 80% of revenue (S047). Lesson: a conviction born of your own pain can be a commercial edge, not a distraction. (S026, S047, S049, S058)

8. **Nap rooms in the city that never sleeps.** HuffPost opened two nap rooms. At first nobody used them for fear of being seen; then they were perpetually booked and she planned a third. Her joke about two people leaving one together: "Whatever helps people recharge just don't tell HR" (S003). Lesson: culture changes when leaders make the behavior safe and visible. (S003, S004, S005, S013, S014, S034, S050)

9. **Selling to AOL for $315M.** She sold HuffPost to AOL in 2011 mainly to fund growth in video, investigative reporting, and international, and stayed to run it until 2016. "We had put $1 million into the Hington Post and we sold it for 315." (S062) She says she sold too soon but would rather sell too soon than too late, and has only good things to say about the deal. Against her CFO and board she insisted on going international through local partners (Le Monde in France), reaching 18 countries quickly. Lesson: sell for a strategic reason, and partner for speed. (S035, S062, S064)

10. **Jack Ma, the carve-out, and the jump to Thrive.** In Hangzhou in 2014, Jack Ma told her to leave HuffPost and build a company on these ideas, and offered to invest. She thought he was "crazy." In 2016 she negotiated 20% of her time from AOL/Verizon to build Thrive on the side, then realized while raising money that she could not do both. Sheryl Sandberg told her to tear up her pros and cons list and jump. Lesson: focus, and big decisions come from the heart. (S025, S035, S043, S049, S053, S062, S063)

11. **Accenture, the first customer before the company existed.** Ellen Shook, Accenture's chief people officer, told her "do it and I will be your first customer" before Thrive had investors or employees. Later, when an Accenture employee developed heart problems, Thrive sent its chief training officer daily and refused to send an invoice. Lesson: treat customers as partners, not vendor relationships. (S062)

12. **The phone bed.** Thrive's first and only physical product was a small bed for phones to charge in outside the bedroom, sold at or below cost on Amazon, because "human beings learn through ritual" (S024). Lesson: a product can be a behavior-change tool first and a profit center never. (S024, S031, S039, S047, S057, S060, S062)

13. **The Uber board.** As the only woman on Uber's board during its 2017 crisis, she oversaw the Eric Holder investigation's 47 recommendations and the CEO search. Over 60% of managers were first-time managers without training, and the values included "always on" and working "harder, smarter, longer." Lesson: "a company has to stand for more than high growth," and burnout culture drives bad behavior; this produced her "compassionate directness" value and "Culture is the immune system of a company." (S030) (S025, S027, S029, S041, S064)

14. **Hiring mistakes made tired.** She audited her own hiring mistakes and found every one was made exhausted: she wanted to cross one more thing off the list and overruled the red flags. At Thrive, early hires who expected to "chill under a mango tree" were wrong for a hyper-growth company. Lesson: never interview tired; be explicit that the job is intense; recover after. (S013, S020, S025, S030, S036, S049, S055, S064)

15. **Bezos and the 5% better decision.** She "bugged him" for months to write about his eight hours of sleep for Thrive. His case: his value depends on the quality of his decisions, and six hours makes them worse. "I may make fewer decisions, but if my decisions are 5% better than they would have been if I was sleep deprived, that's much better for Amazon." (S026) Lesson: role models in the arena give everyone permission. (S026, S028, S042, S064)

16. **Musk, the 2am tweets, and "human energy."** In 2018 she wrote Elon Musk an open letter after the "funding secured" tweet led to an SEC case, his stepping down as chairman, and a $20 million fine. Her line: he understands automotive energy but not human energy. She returns to him in 2025 over "hardcore" office sleepovers. Lesson: exhaustion shows up as governance risk. (S032, S033, S042, S057)

17. **The SVB week.** In March 2023, Thrive had moved its round's money out of Silicon Valley Bank only because another bank offered a better rate. What struck her was how much stress founders spread; she sent one calm all-company email: payroll is fine, no other exposure. Lesson: in a crisis, manage the exposure to stress as well as the exposure to the bank. (S048)

18. **Walmart associates and Thrive AI Health.** Since 2020 Thrive has run a behavior-change program for Walmart associates, with $1M a year in rewards; she tells of associates reversing diabetes and losing large amounts of weight, including one on an 8pm to 8am shift. In 2024 she launched Thrive AI Health with the OpenAI Startup Fund, recruiting a CEO from Google and leaving the product build to him. Lesson: meet people where they are, start with swaps ("can you change the oil you fry it in?"), and use AI for personalization without judgment. (S040, S041, S051, S054, S058, S062, S064)
