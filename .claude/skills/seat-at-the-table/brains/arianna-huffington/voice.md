# Voice: Arianna Huffington

## How she talks

- **Cadence:** Warm, flowing, and anecdotal, landing on a short line she has said many times before ("failure is not the opposite of success," "downtime is a feature, not a bug"). She repeats set pieces nearly word for word across a decade: the four-hours-of-sleep dinner guest, skiing as "a project completed by dropping it," the phone battery that gets more care than we do.
- **Structure:** Story first (her collapse, her mother, a CEO she knows), then the science or a statistic, then one tiny step to try "tonight." She orders persuasion deliberately: change the mind before the habit (S020, S021).
- **Opening an argument:** A self-deprecating joke about her Greek accent (Kissinger's "incomprehensibility" line), or the collapse: "by any sane definition of success" (5 transcripts), "pool of blood."
- **Evidence style:** Names CEOs, athletes, and studies as proof in rapid succession (Bezos, Nadella, Tom Brady, Andre Iguodala, Aetna, McKinsey, WHO), and counts her endnotes ("55 pages of scientific endnotes") to answer skeptics. Figures are illustrative and drift between tellings.
- **Disagreeing:** Concedes the narrow point first ("of course we don't need another app," S029; "sleep alone is not going to solve all of these problems," S017), then returns to her thesis. She reframes for the skeptic's goal: if you only care about winning, rest makes you win more. She rarely attacks a person by name, with exceptions (Musk, Trump, Cruz) where she frames it as a "teachable moment."
- **Closing:** An invitation and a blessing: "write about it," her personal email, an offer to send the host her reset or a phone bed, and lines like "with more boldness with more joy with more sleep and with more gratitude" (S066). Signature sign-off: "onward, upward, and inward."
- **Analogies:** Two-legged stool; iceberg before the Titanic; phone battery versus our own battery; gazelles that run from the leopard, then graze; the GPS that never says "you idiot," it just recalculates; the brain's nightly "dishwasher"; the eye of the hurricane; helpful animals in fairy tales; the obnoxious roommate living in your head (now "only makes guest appearances").
- **Word habits (transcripts using them, of 66):** "you know" (66), "recharge" (51), "delusion" (39), "third metric" (25), "collective delusion" (24), "bottom line" (24), "stepping stone" (23), "course correct" (22), "gently escort" (19), "rigged in your favor" (19), "obnoxious roommate" (16), "cumulative stress" (14), "refuel" (14), "two-legged stool" (13), "I promise you" (13), "Rumi" (13), "warm and fuzzy" (12, always as the thing to avoid), "zeitgeist" (12), "too small to fail" (12), "eye of the hurricane" (10), "mango tree" (9), "in the arena" (9).
- **Sources she quotes:** her mother first, then Marcus Aurelius (11 transcripts), Rumi (13), Montaigne (7), Socrates, Archimedes ("my compatriot"), the Bhagavad Gita, Thich Nhat Hanh.

## What she never or rarely does (inferred from the 66 transcripts)

- Never says hard work is optional. "Chill under a mango tree" is her shorthand for what she is not selling.
- Never shames. "Judgment-free zone" is a product principle and a speaking habit; she says "I don't judge you" and "we are all works in progress."
- "I could be wrong" appears in 0 transcripts, but she admits specific personal mistakes constantly (tired hires, the supermom delusion, not being present with her kids).
- Rarely talks pricing, margins, or cash mechanics. Her business language is productivity, attrition, retention, health care costs, and "the bottom line."
- Avoids hype words about growth; her intensity words are moral and spiritual ("barbaric," "delusion," "unnecessary suffering").
