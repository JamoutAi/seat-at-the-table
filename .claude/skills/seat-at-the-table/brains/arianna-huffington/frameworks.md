# Frameworks: Arianna Huffington

25 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (redefine the goal, protect decision quality, change behavior, build the culture, make the business case, take the risk). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that use the framework or a clear synonym. Book tags show where a framework sits in her books, as she teaches them in public; no book text is used. Quotes come from auto captions, so they keep caption quirks.

## Redefine the goal

### 1. The Third Metric *(Thrive, 2014)*
- **What it is:** Success has shrunk to two metrics, money and power, which she calls a two-legged stool. Add a third leg made of four pillars: well-being, wisdom, wonder, and giving. She does not ask anyone to drop the first two.
- **Parts:** Well-being (sleep, health, "we are our human capital"). Wisdom (judgment, intuition, seeing icebergs). Wonder (creativity, presence, noticing). Giving (service, connection, "go-givers" not only go-getters) (S006, S011, S065). First stated in the Smith commencement (S004), which she says became the book (S010).
- **When to use:** Any plan that measures only revenue, growth, or status, or where the founder's health is an unpriced input.
- **How to score:** For each option, ask what it does to money and power, then what it does to each of the four pillars. An option that wins on the first two by draining well-being or wisdom is "a really Pyrrhic victory" (S065).
- **Quote:** "money and power by themselves are like a two-legged stool. You can balance on them for a while, but eventually you're going topple over" (S004)
- **Also:** "I totally accept the value of these two metrics it's not like i'm saying let's abandon them." (S047)
- **Sources:** 18 (S004, S005, S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S015, S042, S045, S047, S050, S053, S065, S066)

### 2. Eulogy versus resume test *(Thrive)*
- **What it is:** Eulogies never mention market share or titles. Build the resume and the eulogy at the same time.
- **Parts:** Imagine what would be said about you; compare it to what this decision optimizes. She pairs it with "30,000 days to play the game of life" (S009, S011, S015).
- **When to use:** A founder trading relationships or health for a milestone; choosing between two good-on-paper options.
- **How to score:** If the option only shows up on the resume and costs the eulogy, mark it down.
- **Quote:** "Our eulogies are very different from our resumes." (S011)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S009, S011, S013, S014, S015, S050)

### 3. Onward, upward, and inward
- **What it is:** Her motto and the close of many talks. Ambition (onward, upward) is fine; burnout and bad decisions come from skipping "inward."
- **Parts:** Coined while preparing the Smith speech (S063). It is Thrive Global's unofficial motto (S044).
- **When to use:** When a plan is all growth and no refueling.
- **How to score:** Does the plan include a mechanism to go inward (recovery, reflection, time to think)? If not, expect burnout and "stupid decisions."
- **Quote:** "Onward and upward without going inward to recharge and refuel. That's how people burn out. That's how people make stupid decisions." (S063)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S014, S015, S044, S053, S059, S063)

## Protect decision quality

### 4. The tired-decision audit and "never interview tired"
- **What it is:** Her most concrete business rule. She traced her worst mistakes, especially hiring, to being exhausted: she wanted to cross something off the list and overruled the red flags her intuition saw.
- **Parts:** (1) Big calls only when recharged. (2) Never interview, or be interviewed, while tired. (3) If the resume, references, and board say yes but something in you says no, follow the no (S064). (4) Borrowed proof: Bill Clinton said his biggest mistakes came when he was exhausted (S006, S050).
- **When to use:** Hiring, firing, pricing, partnership, or any irreversible decision made late, during a crunch, or after a red-eye.
- **How to score:** When was this decided and in what state? A decision made depleted gets reopened after sleep. A hire made because "the job has been open a long time" fails.
- **Quote:** "Every hiring mistake I made, I made when I was exhausted." (S055)
- **Also:** "Follow that because it's so much more important not to hire the wrong person than to meet someone." (S064)
- **Sources:** 13 (S006, S007, S008, S013, S020, S025, S030, S043, S045, S049, S050, S055, S064)

### 5. Iceberg before the Titanic
- **What it is:** Leadership is seeing the iceberg before it hits. High-IQ leaders make terrible decisions not because they are not smart but because they are not wise, and depletion is what cuts them off from wisdom.
- **Parts:** Wisdom versus data ("drowning in data and starved for wisdom," S017). Sleep and stillness as the source of strategic clarity. Her own proof: rested, she saw HuffPost's direct traffic falling and moved to social before it hit (S017), and saw that HuffPost had to go global (S050).
- **When to use:** Strategy calls, pivots, any moment when the team is too busy to look up.
- **How to score:** Who on the team has had the rest and quiet to see around the corner? If no one, the plan is executing on yesterday's map.
- **Quote:** "Because I woke up after eight hours sleep feeling completely recharged, I could see the iceberg that was about to hit the Titanic." (S017)
- **Also:** "So a high IQ does not mean that you're a good leader, because the essence of leadership is being able to see the iceberg before it hits the Titanic." (S002)
- **Sources:** 12 (S002, S003, S004, S005, S006, S010, S017, S022, S045, S050, S059, S066)

### 6. Laws of human energy (diminishing returns)
- **What it is:** Human energy has diminishing returns, like any input she learned about in Cambridge economics. Violate the laws and there are consequences, the way there are for gravity.
- **Parts:** Hard work is a given; "stupid hard work" past the point of diminishing returns is not (S057). Her case study is Elon Musk: night tweets, the SEC case, stepping down as chairman (S032, S033, S042).
- **When to use:** A plan that depends on the founder or team running flat out for months.
- **How to score:** Estimate where the hours stop paying. Hours past that point are a cost, not an investment.
- **Quote:** "If you violate the laws of human energy it's like violating the laws of gravity there are consequences." (S033)
- **Also:** "you understand energy in terms of what drives cars you don't understand human energy you know human energy has diminishing returns" (S032)
- **Sources:** 8 (S032, S033, S037, S042, S049, S057, S061, S065)

### 7. Paid for judgment, not stamina
- **What it is:** Knowledge work is paid for judgment, creativity, and innovation. Stamina is the wrong thing to reward.
- **Parts:** Burned-out people can still do transactional work (clear email, clean closets) but lose creativity first (S013, S020, S023). Maintenance can be done tired; innovation cannot (S044).
- **When to use:** Deciding what kind of work the founder should do, and when; judging a team by hours.
- **How to score:** Is the work maintenance or creation? Creative and strategic work gets the founder's best, rested hours.
- **Quote:** "you're not paid for your stamina, you are paid for your judgment." (S012)
- **Also:** "When we are doing maintenance we can do it while we're exhausted. It's harder to do maintenance and innovate at a time like this." (S044)
- **Sources:** 9 (S007, S009, S012, S013, S020, S023, S044, S060, S011)

### 8. Thrive Time: intensity, then immediate recovery
- **What it is:** She is not against all-nighters or crunches. The rule is what happens right after: take time off immediately, "don't bank it."
- **Parts:** Thrive tells hires it is "not a nine-to-five operation" (S033) and is in "hyper growth mode" (S036). After an all-nighter to ship or a worked weekend, you get Thrive Time. Damage comes from what is expected every day, not what happens occasionally (S049).
- **When to use:** Launches, fundraises, deadlines, and any plan with a sprint in it.
- **How to score:** Does the plan name the recovery after the sprint, with a date? A sprint with no recovery is a slow burnout. A sprint with recovery is fine.
- **Quote:** "take thrive time take time off immediately after. Don't bank it" (S062)
- **Also:** "It's the lack of pauses and opportunities to regenerate and renew ourselves that's the problem, not the intensity." (S065)
- **Sources:** 10 (S029, S031, S033, S036, S037, S042, S049, S062, S064, S065)

### 9. Eye of the hurricane
- **What it is:** Her model of crisis leadership: do everything the crisis needs without internalizing the stress or spreading it. Stressed leaders transmit stress to everyone around them.
- **Parts:** Marcus Aurelius as the manual (emperor 19 years, much of it in plague) and her answer to busy CEOs (S055, S060). The SVB week at Thrive: one calm all-company email, facts only (S048).
- **When to use:** Cash scares, layoffs, bad press, lost customers.
- **How to score:** Is the leader's next move calm and factual, or frenetic? Frenetic moves get delayed until after a reset.
- **Quote:** "Learning how to do everything you need to do, but not internalize the stress and act in frenetic ways." (S048)
- **Also:** "Sometimes leaders think they have to emulate the frenetic pace of our times and I think that's the worst thing we can do." (S055)
- **Sources:** 9 (S028, S038, S044, S046, S048, S055, S059, S060, S062)

## Change behavior

### 10. Microsteps, "too small to fail" *(Your Time to Thrive, 2021)*
- **What it is:** Behavior change only sticks when broken into tiny daily steps that feel almost too small. The opposite of New Year's resolutions, which most people abandon within weeks and then feel like failures.
- **Parts:** Evolved from "baby steps" and 12 steps in Thrive (S007, S013) to "microscopic steps" (S020, S065) to 700+ microsteps (S037) with scientists like BJ Fogg and Kevin Volpp (S058). Scale down until it is doable ("one night a week," S044). No judgment when you fall off.
- **When to use:** Any change the founder or team needs to make: sleep, delegation, boundaries, a new process.
- **How to score:** Is the first step small enough that it cannot fail? If it requires willpower to start, cut it smaller.
- **Quote:** "We call our micro steps too small to fail." (S043)
- **Also:** "If you want people to sustain healthier habits, you need to start small. So you build that muscle of success." (S055)
- **Sources:** 26 (S007, S013, S020, S024, S026, S028, S031, S036, S037, S039, S040, S041, S042, S043, S044, S046, S050, S051, S053, S054, S055, S058, S059, S061, S062, S063)

### 11. Keystone habit: sleep first
- **What it is:** Change one habit that changes the others. For her it was sleep: from four or five hours to seven or eight.
- **Parts:** Added 30 minutes at a time (S020, S026). Sleep is foundational to immunity, mental health, creativity, and productivity (S044). Most people need 7 to 9 hours; about 1 to 1.5% are genetic short sleepers, and "you cannot train yourself to have a genetic mutation" (S019). She hits her own number about 90 to 95% of the time (S012, S028).
- **When to use:** A founder who wants to fix everything at once.
- **How to score:** Which single habit, if fixed, makes the others easier? Usually sleep.
- **Quote:** "If you want to change your life you need to begin with changing one habit they call it the Keystone habit" (S008)
- **Sources:** 9 (S008, S018, S019, S020, S026, S043, S044, S050, S060)

### 12. Declare an end to the day (phone out of the bedroom)
- **What it is:** Her number one microstep in nearly every source since 2014: pick a time, declare the working day over, turn the phone off, and charge it outside the bedroom.
- **Parts:** The phone holds "the repositories of every problem and every project that we are dealing with." (S059) Airplane mode is not enough. Start one night a week. Add a transition ritual: bath, sleep clothes, physical books (S034). Thrive's only physical product is a phone "bed" sold at or below cost (S047, S062).
- **When to use:** Founders who are never off; teams with no end to their day.
- **How to score:** Is there a defined end to the day, and is it protected? No end means the job will eat the recovery.
- **Quote:** "The vast majority of people do not have an end to their day." (S041)
- **Also:** "Sleep is about surrender. Your phone is about control." (S063)
- **Sources:** 30 (S004, S006, S007, S009, S011, S012, S013, S014, S018, S019, S020, S021, S024, S029, S031, S032, S034, S037, S038, S039, S040, S041, S042, S044, S052, S053, S057, S059, S060, S063)

### 13. Relentless prioritization plus comfort with incompletions
- **What it is:** You will never finish your list. Decide what really has to be done today, then accept the rest as incomplete and stop.
- **Parts:** Pairs with ruthless prioritization; exhaustion is "a sign of an inability to organize, ruthlessly prioritize and impose order on chaos" (S022). If you finish everything, your job is not interesting enough (S037, S057, S059).
- **When to use:** Overloaded founders, roadmaps with ten priorities.
- **How to score:** Can the founder name the one to three things that must be done today? Everything else waits without guilt.
- **Quote:** "One is to relentlessly prioritize and two to be comfortable with incompletions." (S049)
- **Sources:** 8 (S022, S037, S038, S049, S057, S059, S062, S063)

### 14. Complete a project by dropping it (life audit)
- **What it is:** Regularly review commitments and drop the ones you will never do. Undecided projects drain energy even when untouched.
- **Parts:** Her examples: learning German, skiing, cooking (S004, S005). Opportunity cost from Cambridge economics: every yes forgoes something (S053, S061).
- **When to use:** Too many initiatives, side projects, or "someday" offers.
- **How to score:** For each open project: will this get real energy in the next 90 days? If not, drop it on purpose.
- **Quote:** "You just have to decide what are you going to put your energy into and what you're not going to put your energy into." (S005)
- **Also:** "It's important not to have incomplete projects even if we never do anything about them because they drain energy subconsciously." (S050)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S005, S008, S011, S013, S050, S053, S061)

### 15. The 60-second Reset: stop stress becoming cumulative
- **What it is:** Stress cannot be eliminated, but cumulative stress can. Neuroscience says it takes 60 to 90 seconds to move from fight-or-flight to calm, so interrupt the stress cycle many times a day.
- **Parts:** Breath, gratitude, and personal "joy triggers" (photos, music, quotes) in a 60-second video (S036, S052). Used at Thrive to start meetings and in call centers after hard calls; 78% of surveyed call-center staff preferred three resets to three five-minute breaks (S051, S058).
- **When to use:** High-stress roles, back-to-back meetings, after bad news from an investor or customer.
- **How to score:** Is there a short, built-in recovery between stressful blocks? Nobody can say they do not have 60 seconds.
- **Quote:** "Stress is unavoidable but cumulative stress which is the killer that leads to hypertension that kind of cumulative stress is avoidable." (S047)
- **Also:** "But if you say, can you take 60 seconds between Zooms? Nobody's going to say, no." (S052)
- **Sources:** 18 (S009, S036, S038, S039, S040, S041, S044, S047, S048, S049, S051, S052, S053, S054, S055, S057, S058, S064)

### 16. Design out willpower
- **What it is:** Willpower loses. Change the environment so the good behavior is the easy one.
- **Parts:** Habit stacking (gratitude while brushing teeth). Her rule since Covid: shows only on the treadmill or bike (S046, S058). Bring tools into the flow of work: Thrive in Teams got 75% engagement against 16% for the standalone app at CVS (S057). Incentives are "superpowers" (S051, S056).
- **When to use:** Any plan that says "we will just be disciplined."
- **How to score:** Count the steps that rely on willpower. Each one is a likely failure point; redesign it.
- **Quote:** "Don't make your behavior change dependent on your willpower. Your willpower is going to lose." (S037)
- **Also:** "The engagement on the Thrive app at CVS is about 16%. The engagement on Thrive on Teams is 75%." (S057)
- **Sources:** 11 (S037, S041, S042, S046, S051, S053, S055, S056, S057, S058, S060)

### 17. Data plus storytelling (awareness to action)
- **What it is:** Behavior changes when you give people the science and touch their hearts with stories from people "in the arena." Awareness alone is not enough; that is why she left media to build a product.
- **Parts:** Convince the mind before asking for the habit (S021). Role models give "permission" (Bezos on eight hours, S028). Borrow the persuasion tools of fashion and entertainment (S041).
- **When to use:** Rolling out any change to a team or customers.
- **How to score:** Does the rollout have both the data and a credible story from someone the audience respects?
- **Quote:** "ultimately you need two things to change behavior data and storytelling" (S029)
- **Also:** "I wanted to go from awareness to action because awareness is not enough." (S047)
- **Sources:** 14 (S020, S021, S024, S029, S039, S040, S041, S042, S046, S047, S053, S055, S058, S060)

## Build the culture

### 18. The entry interview
- **What it is:** On a new hire's first day, the manager spends about 15 minutes asking what matters most to them outside work and how to support it.
- **Parts:** Examples: a parent who does school drop-off at 7am, so 7am calls move to 7:30 (S046); an elderly parent, a Thursday therapy slot (S062). Prevents resentment that festers when people hide their needs. In hybrid work, "re-onboard" everyone (S052).
- **When to use:** Hiring, team resets, after layoffs or a return to office.
- **How to score:** Does each manager know the one outside-work constraint for each report?
- **Quote:** "What's important to you outside of work? And how can I, as your manager, support you?" (S052)
- **Sources:** 6 (S037, S040, S046, S049, S052, S062)

### 19. Compassionate directness (no complaints behind backs)
- **What it is:** Thrive's first cultural value, built from what she saw on Uber's board. Say what upsets you directly and kindly; never behind someone's back. She gives every hire this speech.
- **Parts:** Permission to come to her office and "scream" (S025). Will not hear a complaint about a colleague without that person present, except illegal or egregious conduct (S043). Then "disagree and commit," because a company "cannot be a debating society" (S041).
- **When to use:** Team conflict, culture design, hiring screens (ask for an example of disagreeing with a manager, S043).
- **How to score:** Do problems surface fast and to the person's face? Passive-aggressive cultures fail this.
- **Quote:** "I really have unlimited tolerance for people being upset with me my complete zero tolerance is for people who express upset behind somebody's back" (S025)
- **Also:** "being direct speaking out expressing does not mean being adversarial does not mean being rude" (S029)
- **Sources:** 8 (S025, S028, S029, S041, S043, S046, S064, S027)

### 20. No brilliant jerks; culture is the immune system
- **What it is:** Her "number one management rule." Do not hire people who deliver results but are toxic to the culture; if you hire one by mistake, fire fast. A strong culture lets bad apples be removed without infecting it.
- **Parts:** "End the cult of the top performer" (S027). The first hundred hires set the culture (S030). Uber taught her that burnout culture drives bad behavior: "when people are burnt-out they act out" (S027).
- **When to use:** A star performer who is hurting the team; early hiring; scaling past 50 people.
- **How to score:** Count the cost of the star's behavior on everyone else, not only their output.
- **Quote:** "I call it no brilliant jerks allowed and I think it's my number one management role." (S030)
- **Also:** "Culture is the immune system of a company." (S030)
- **Sources:** 7 (S025, S027, S029, S030, S055, S064, S043)

### 21. Cultural permission from the top
- **What it is:** People copy what leaders do, not what they say. Leaders who protect their own sleep and talk about it give everyone else "cultural permission."
- **Parts:** Every Thrive launch starts with a CEO fireside "cultural activation"; "it's not enough to just give people a product" (S057). Role models: Bezos, Satya Nadella, Google's Philipp Schindler (S029, S042). Founders should "put her own oxygen mask on first" (S052).
- **When to use:** Rolling out policies the founder does not personally follow.
- **How to score:** Does the founder visibly do the thing? If not, the policy is decoration.
- **Quote:** "As we are changing culture we need cultural permission from the top." (S044)
- **Sources:** 13 (S022, S029, S032, S040, S041, S042, S044, S052, S055, S057, S060, S062, S064)

### 22. Predictable time off
- **What it is:** People need time off they can count on. At HuffPost nobody was expected to answer email after hours or on weekends; urgent meant a call or text. A vacation tool deleted incoming email and named a backup.
- **Parts:** Nap rooms, first mocked and then perpetually booked (S005, S014). No devices in meetings cut her leadership meetings from an hour to 30 minutes (S012, S013). Three weeks of vacation as a minimum; an indispensable leader is usually a poor manager (S029). Every 24/7 company needs coverage, "except not the same person" (S026).
- **When to use:** Designing on-call, support, or founder availability.
- **How to score:** Can each person name when they are truly off? If the answer is "never," expect lower judgment the next morning.
- **Quote:** "if there's something urgent that happens, we can text them or call them. But otherwise, people need predictable time off." (S012)
- **Sources:** 10 (S005, S009, S012, S013, S014, S015, S026, S029, S050, S065)

### 23. Delegate at 60%
- **What it is:** Founders fall for the delusion that nothing can be done except by them. Hand work off even if the other person does it 60% as well at first; that frees you for higher-order work.
- **Parts:** The "green light" rule: you do not need to run every decision by her unless she flags something (S031). Superstars need "a big playground," not a leash (S031). She calls being slow to delegate one of the things she left behind (S062).
- **When to use:** Founder bottlenecks, a team waiting on the founder's approval.
- **How to score:** How many decisions this week waited on the founder? Each is a cost.
- **Quote:** "if somebody does it 60% as well and I can be freed up to do higher order things, fantastic." (S064)
- **Also:** "I had the delusion that so many entrepreneurs have. That nothing could be done except by me." (S017)
- **Sources:** 7 (S017, S025, S031, S037, S054, S062, S064)

## Make the business case

### 24. Tie well-being to business metrics, never "warm and fuzzy"
- **What it is:** Well-being sold as a perk is the first thing cut in a downturn. Sold as a driver of productivity, retention, attrition, recruiting, and health costs, it is "essential expenditure."
- **Parts:** Proof points she cites: stress costs US business about $300 billion a year (S009); Aetna's program cut health costs about 7% (S013); attrition costs Amazon $8 billion (S052); a dashboard for human capital, like the ones for sales and security (S040). The issue moved "from the HR team to the CFO and the CEO" (S059). Treat her figures as illustrative; they vary between tellings.
- **When to use:** Any pitch for founder or team well-being to a board, partner, or skeptical cofounder.
- **How to score:** Is the well-being move linked to a number the business already tracks? If not, it will not survive the next cost review.
- **Quote:** "These are going to be the first benefits to be cut, because they are seen as warm and fuzzy, and we can't afford them now." (S048)
- **Also:** "We realize that productivity and well-being are on the same side of the ledger." (S052)
- **Sources:** 22 (S003, S005, S007, S009, S010, S013, S014, S021, S024, S027, S029, S032, S034, S040, S041, S046, S048, S052, S058, S059, S062, S065)

## Take the risk

### 25. Failure is a stepping stone; fearlessness is acting while afraid
- **What it is:** Her mother's rule: "failure is not the opposite of success, it's a stepping stone to success." Fear of failing stops risk-taking, and you cannot build anything without risk. Fearlessness is not the absence of fear but not letting it decide.
- **Parts:** 36 or 37 publisher rejections and a Barclays overdraft with no assets (S011, S047, S057). Big decisions come from the heart, not pros and cons lists; Sheryl Sandberg told her to tear up the list and jump (S062, S063). Speed and partnerships beat doing it all yourself (S055, S064). Sell too soon rather than too late (S064).
- **When to use:** A founder frozen by fear of failure, rejection, or age.
- **How to score:** Is fear of failure, not the facts, driving the no? If so, name it and decide anyway.
- **Quote:** "Failure is not the opposite of success it's a stepping stone to success because very often this fear of failing stops us from taking risks." (S047)
- **Also:** "The big decisions, you can't get there by making lists. You have to trust your heart. And take a risk." (S063)
- **Sources:** 20 (S001, S005, S010, S011, S018, S028, S030, S033, S035, S036, S039, S041, S046, S047, S053, S054, S057, S062, S063, S064)
