# Frameworks: Leila Hormozi

24 frameworks, grouped by the job they do (get the right people, set them up, hold the standard, get the founder out of the way, let people go, decide). "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus that teach the framework or a clear version of it. She has not published a book yet (one on leadership is in progress, S011, S052), so there are no book tags. Quotes come from auto captions and keep caption quirks.

## Get the right people

### 1. Two funnels: talent acquisition mirrors client acquisition
- **What it is:** A business has one funnel that gets customers and one that gets employees. Lead generation becomes application generation, lead nurture becomes applicant nurture, and sales becomes interviewing. The bigger the company, the more founder time should move from the customer funnel to the talent funnel.
- **Parts:** Job title written as an ad headline, in the words the candidate uses for themselves, not internal language (no "Rockstar," no abbreviations, no pay in the title) (S022, S026). Six channels: network, cold outreach (her favorite for senior or hard roles), paid ads (high-volume roles), content and brand, recruiters, team referrals (S022). Applicant nurture: read every resume, screen out job hoppers and people who never moved up; of 100 applicants she screens 7 to 9 and 2 to 3 reach full interviews (S022, S026). Candidate experience: same-day or next-day speed, volume so the choice is hard, personal messages, omnichannel follow-up (S022). For one-off senior roles, spray and pray across every channel; go deep on one channel only for roles you hire repeatedly (S010). Map: job description is the sales page, interview is the sales call, onboarding is fulfillment, retention is LTV (S060).
- **When to use:** "We can't find good people," a stalled hire, any plan that needs headcount to execute.
- **How to score:** Does the plan name where candidates come from and how many applicants a role needs? If the only channel is the founder's network or one Indeed post, mark it down: small pools force compromise.
- **Quote:** "There are two funnels in your business. One gets customers, one gets employees." (S016)
- **Also:** "if you can market and find customers then you can market and recruit Talent it is literally the same skill set" (S022)
- **Sources:** 9 (S010, S016, S022, S026, S048, S055, S059, S060, S063)

### 2. Employee value proposition and company talent match
- **What it is:** Just as you put an offer into the customer marketplace, you put an offer into the talent marketplace. Company talent match is the degree to which your company satisfies what top talent wants.
- **Parts:** Five parts: compensation (salary, raise and promotion path, fairness, transparency), benefits (time off, flexibility, remote or in person, education), career (progression, training, feedback), work environment (autonomy, clarity, recognition), culture (mission, values, "internal branding") (S048, S059). Pick what you lead with: money, growth, or status. Hers: growth first, status second, pay at the top end but not the lead (S012, S016). Build a vision big enough for people's own visions to fit inside (S003, S059). You compete with every option the person has, including staying put under a mediocre boss (S007, S016).
- **When to use:** Hiring is slow, offers get declined, or good people leave after a year.
- **How to score:** Can the founder say in one sentence why a top performer should work here instead of a better-funded competitor? If the only answer is pay, the EVP is weak.
- **Quote:** "You have to know your employee value proposition right just like you have an offer for the marketplace you have to have an offer for the talent Marketplace." (S012)
- **Also:** "They will take a better boss than they will better pay any day." (S016)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S007, S012, S016, S048, S059, S060, S022)

### 3. The three C's of hiring: character, competence, commitment
- **What it is:** Her hiring filter. Character is fit with this company's values, shown in past behavior. Competence is the baseline skill to do the job, tested. Commitment is what the person is willing to restrict to do this job.
- **Parts:** Character: "operationalize your values" into behavior questions (a time you were honest and it hurt someone; your proudest career moment) (S030, S012). Competence: hire for skill, not experience; break the job into its top five tasks and test each with a work sample, role play, or a specific past story (S030). Commitment: what did they give up to get here; leaders must be fully committed, no side businesses (S030). If they cannot tell a detailed story about doing the thing, they are probably making it up (S030).
- **When to use:** Any hire, especially leaders.
- **How to score:** Rate each C separately. A strong resume with a weak character score is a no. A leader with a side business scores low on commitment.
- **Quote:** "behavior is the proxy for values what you want to see is how they act" (S030)
- **Also:** "skill without commitment is untapped potential you can have somebody with all the best character in the world" (S030)
- **Sources:** 6 (S009, S012, S030, S047, S057, S060)

### 4. The interview gauntlet
- **What it is:** A minimum multi-step interview process with a script and an objective for each stage, plus a scorecard so likability does not decide.
- **Parts:** Phone screen (are they sane, do the basics hold) (S040). Interview 1: history, qualifications, values. Interview 2: skills, with a real problem from last quarter (S040, S022). Culture and alignment interview: what they want, compensation preference, what work from home means to them (S022). Gatekeeper interview by the CEO, protecting culture and the candidate's own interests: tell them the bad parts (S040). A weighted scorecard of must-haves (S011). Take 20 interviews for a role to build pattern recognition (S010). Ask candidates to state what their first 30, 60, 90 days look like (S063). Read the team's red flags to the candidate in the final round and watch who gets defensive (S001).
- **When to use:** Every hire; senior hires get more steps, not fewer.
- **How to score:** Count the stages and whether each has a script. No script means no reliable outcomes.
- **Quote:** "If you don't have a script and you don't have a process you would not get reliable outcomes" (S026)
- **Also:** "when you interview somebody they're pitching you they're selling you this is the best you're ever going to see" (S030)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S010, S011, S022, S026, S030, S040, S047, S063)

### 5. Hiring biases and early red flags
- **What it is:** The traps that make founders hire the wrong people, and the signals in the first weeks that a hire is wrong.
- **Parts:** Five biases: confirmation (decide in 15 minutes, then justify), expectation anchors (hire a copy of the last person or yourself), halo (one shiny school or employer), similarity, affinity (S047). Likeness blinds you (S063, S011). Remote red flags in week one: slow to respond, no good news from the team, camera off, sick days right away, dress out of step, tech not set up; when you see one, pull the thread (S033). Fix: lead with curiosity, use multiple interviewers, and if you really like someone, have others interview them (S047).
- **When to use:** Before a final offer, and in a new hire's first 30 days.
- **How to score:** Was the decision made by one person who liked the candidate? Mark it down. Any week-one red flag that nobody investigated scores against the hire.
- **Quote:** "People that are very likable usually get by by being likable, not by being competent." (S011)
- **Also:** "Your gut's going to lead you to building a team that's just a bunch of replicas of who you already are." (S047)
- **Sources:** 6 (S011, S030, S033, S047, S063, S026)

### 6. When to hire: time-cost rule, the three P's, and capacity before the bottleneck
- **What it is:** Rules for whether to add a full-time person at all, and when.
- **Parts:** First hires: hire for what takes the most of your time and costs the least to hire (support, billing), not "what do I hate" (S014). The three P's: is the gap a person, a process, or a project? Use a contractor, surge support, or a 3 to 6 month project first (S053). Do not hire ahead of demand; stretch people briefly to confirm the hire is real (S014). Do not hire before consistent revenue (S027). But build the four capacities (financial, personnel, systems, thinking) in excess before a known launch (S016). Never let inexperienced managers make hiring projections (S016). Exception: hire the project manager before demand is steady if the founder is stuck doing fulfillment (S018). Hiring into a broken or missing system adds headcount without capacity (S060).
- **When to use:** Any plan that adds headcount, and any launch.
- **How to score:** Is the hire tied to proven demand or a dated launch with capacity planned? Who made the forecast? A forecast from a first-time manager scores against.
- **Quote:** "A full-time employee is a one-size fits-all solution to a very intricate problem which is how do you meet the demand of a growing business." (S053)
- **Also:** "For anything to be successful, you need those four types of capacity in excess before it launches." (S016)
- **Sources:** 8 (S014, S016, S018, S027, S035, S053, S058, S060)

### 7. Pay design: top of market or pay for performance
- **What it is:** How to pay so you attract the right people and repel the wrong ones, without locking in fixed cost you will regret.
- **Parts:** If you can, pay top of market (S045). If you cannot, lower base and higher variable: KPI bonuses, retention bonuses, per-output pay for creatives (S045, S007). The more senior the role, the more base is traded for performance pay; an example CTO base of 450,000 with an uncapped bonus tied to growth and profit (S059). Executives: never full equity on day one; a one-year cliff, then vesting, with cash incentives tied to both revenue and profit (S055). Raises on objective performance, not tenure (S059). Do not set early pay from today's spare cash; it is very hard to pay someone less later (S024). Perks that cost effort, not money: flexibility, development, education (S045).
- **When to use:** Making an offer, building a comp plan, or deciding raises in a tight year.
- **How to score:** Is senior pay tied to results? Is fixed labor cost affordable if revenue dips 30%? A flat salary for a creative or seller scores low.
- **Quote:** "The higher up in the company somebody is the more I'm going to trade base salary with performance salary." (S059)
- **Also:** "if you pay people now based on the cash you have available now you screw yourself in two and three and four years" (S024)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S024, S025, S045, S055, S059, S022)

## Set them up to win

### 8. Onboarding as acceleration
- **What it is:** Onboarding is time to value for an employee, the same idea as for a customer. Most failed hires were failed onboardings.
- **Parts:** It starts when the offer is signed: timeline, logins, swag (S039). Five C's: compliance, clarity (30/60/90 goals), culture immersion, connection (a buddy, sit in on other teams), check back (S039). She talks to new hires twice a day for the first two weeks, then tapers; executives write their own 30/60/90 for her review (S039, S052). Batch-onboard repeat roles with a seven-day immersive accelerator; design it by asking what your best early people already had and what you successfully taught them (S018, S055). Set expectations about 5% worse than reality, so reality delights (S049). Onboarding should mirror the support the role will get later (S049). Re-onboard before firing (S039).
- **When to use:** Every new hire; any hire who is struggling in the first 90 days.
- **How to score:** Is there a written first-two-weeks plan and a named person checking in daily? If not, expect the hire to fail and blame them.
- **Quote:** "almost every time somebody has not succeeded in one of my companies, especially in the very beginning, it's because they've had poor onboarding." (S039)
- **Also:** "when I think of onboarding I think of not onboarding but acceleration for an employee" (S018)
- **Sources:** 6 (S018, S039, S049, S052, S055, S057)

### 9. Four levels of expectations
- **What it is:** Expectations flow down a pyramid, each level built from the one above, so everyone knows how to win the game.
- **Parts:** (1) Core values. (2) Brand promise: results times experience, a promise you can hit 100% of the time. (3) Departmental expectations and KPIs. (4) Role expectations. Worked example from Gym Launch: value "speed is king," promise of questions answered in 5 minutes or less, department KPI of inquiries answered under 120 seconds, role standard of 80% under 120 seconds (S029, S050). Roll it out one level at a time; people need to hear something seven times (S029). Every job description has a results section (S060).
- **When to use:** A team that looks busy but misses goals, or a new department.
- **How to score:** Can a frontline person say what number they are judged on and how it connects to the company promise? If not, start at the level that is missing.
- **Quote:** "Lack of clear expectations leads to assumptions, assumptions lead to unmet goals." (S050)
- **Also:** "if you don't have a scoreboard for each department how the hell do they know if they're winning the game" (S029)
- **Sources:** 5 (S029, S049, S050, S060, S027)

### 10. Values as behaviors (three values, enforced)
- **What it is:** Values are constraints on how you reach the vision and filters for decisions. They only exist if they are visible in behavior and reinforced.
- **Parts:** Keep to three so you can decide fast; hers are sincere candor, unimpeachable character, competitive greatness (S005, S050). Choose them with the founding team: favorite traits, an "alien test," then test whether those three filters alone would hire the right people (S029, S057). Define each as observable behavior; you must be able to observe what you enforce (S060). Test them in interviews by asking people to show, not tell (S012). Reinforce through recognition, consequences, and who you hire and fire (S025, S060). Values beat rules: rules create rule followers (S050).
- **When to use:** Any opportunity, hire, or partner that tempts you off-mission; any culture complaint.
- **How to score:** Does the option fit all three values? "Even if it's a great opportunity, it's not our opportunity" (S009) when it does not.
- **Quote:** "Values are basically constraints of how you get to the vision." (S009)
- **Also:** "most companies what they do is they put a lot of work into deciding what the values are and they put zero work into actually upholding them" (S025)
- **Sources:** 11 (S005, S009, S012, S025, S028, S029, S030, S050, S057, S060, S062)

### 11. Communication and meeting cadence
- **What it is:** Communication is the bridge from confusion to clarity, and meeting cadence is the heartbeat of the team.
- **Parts:** Her formula: energy times engagement plus emotional control times exploration (S043). Three CEO types: Sally Says Nothing, Tony Terrible, Evie Effective (S043). Meeting cadence by the three P's: pulse (activity times size), purpose (strategic or tactical, at the right frequency), preparation (information plus the right people, over an agenda of decisions) (S046). Review last month's meetings every month and cut what is useless (S046). Repeat the mission on purpose, especially remote: quarterly vision talk, weekly CEO update (S025). Share context; hoarded context makes a weaker company (S025, S049). Communication will always be the top complaint after 10 to 30 people (S053, S057).
- **When to use:** Silos, surprises, missed handoffs, or a growing team.
- **How to score:** Is there a set weekly, monthly, and quarterly rhythm, and does the plan say who will be told what? No rhythm scores low.
- **Quote:** "there's no risk to over communicating there's a risk to under communicating" (S043)
- **Also:** "imagine if everybody else in the company had as much context as you do you think they could make better decisions for the company" (S025)
- **Sources:** 8 (S025, S032, S043, S046, S049, S053, S057, S065)

## Hold the standard

### 12. The accountability formula and the chief accountability officer
- **What it is:** Accountability means holding people responsible for actions and results. The CEO is the strongest source of it; you do not delegate it, you disseminate it.
- **Parts:** (Expectations + Measurement) x Feedback = Accountability; feedback is the only multiplier (S042). Tactical accountability (on time, cameras on, reports) is about 20% and safe; developmental (habits, character, career growth) is about 80% and hard (S031). Symptoms of low accountability: low revenue per head, high churn, lots of complaining, 50 open projects (S042). The lies that stop it: "I shouldn't have to babysit," "they're experienced," "no time" (S031, S042). Hold executives to the same standard as the front line (S031). Delegate and follow up, never delegate and forget (S053).
- **When to use:** Missed deadlines, a resented employee, a department that underperforms.
- **How to score:** Before blaming the person: were the expectation and the measurement set, and has anyone given feedback in the last two weeks? If not, the gap is the leader's.
- **Quote:** "the thing that you have to understand is that you don't delegate this you disseminate this" (S042)
- **Also:** "if i'm angry at someone on my team if i feel resentment or like i want to say something not nice about them it's usually because i'm not doing my job" (S042)
- **Sources:** 7 (S010, S031, S041, S042, S050, S053, S060)

### 13. End-of-week reports and self-measurement
- **What it is:** People measure themselves weekly against the expectation, and the leader responds to every line, so the habit sticks.
- **Parts:** A weekly report covering metrics, projects, and personal progress, submitted Thursday; she reviews them every Friday early morning and comments on every section, and has for about eight years (S010, S020). Hold up the mirror: "how do you think you're doing?" and lead with ask before you assume (S010). People who name their own gaps act on them (S019). Asking for data and ignoring it is punishing (S020).
- **When to use:** Any team over about five people, remote teams, and new managers.
- **How to score:** Is there a weekly self-report, and does someone senior read and answer it? A report nobody reads scores as zero.
- **Quote:** "if you don't respond to the reports they will stop filling them out that's just the science of behavior" (S020)
- **Also:** "You then put in the mechanism where someone can evaluate themselves against the expectation and then you just hold up the mirror." (S010)
- **Sources:** 5 (S010, S019, S020, S053, S060)

### 14. Feedback: critique not insult, anchored to their goal
- **What it is:** Feedback names the gap between where someone is and where the goal is, ties it to what that person wants, and tells them what to do instead.
- **Parts:** Critique states the gap; an insult labels the person (S001, S018, S054, S065). Learn each person's goal and anchor to it, for example by asking how a COO would have handled it, if that is their goal (S051, S056). Give a script, not a skill (S001). One piece of feedback, not 12; more frequent feedback should be smaller (S018, S019). Feedback Fridays for new managers (S053). Reward bad news: her first words are "thank you so much for telling me" (S011). Ask: do you want them to feel bad, or get better? (S001)
- **When to use:** Every one-on-one; any underperformance before it becomes a firing.
- **How to score:** Does the plan include who gives feedback, how often, and against what goal? Feedback only at annual reviews scores low.
- **Quote:** "An insult is when you associate with somebody with something negative versus a critique is telling them the gap between where they are and where the ideal is." (S001)
- **Also:** "It's the main like takeaway is tell them what to do instead." (S012)
- **Sources:** 11 (S001, S011, S012, S016, S018, S019, S051, S053, S054, S056, S065)

### 15. Hard conversations: the six E's and four A's
- **What it is:** Her script for the conversation you are avoiding. She calls it the number one skill a CEO can have.
- **Parts:** Six E's, before they speak: establish tone, eliminate assumptions (say up front whether their job is at risk), explain the goal, explain the origin, emphasize the impact, estimate severity on a 1 to 10 scale. Four A's, after: ask for their view, advise, agree on a plan and cadence, assure them where they stand (S032). Prep: put time between the emotion and the talk, write it out, record and review like game tape (S032). Use the coach frame: "you're at a six, I need you at a 10" (S016).
- **When to use:** Behavior problems (90% of hard conversations are about attitude and behavior, not KPIs), conflicts, demotions, firings.
- **How to score:** Has the conversation happened, in direct words, with a follow-up date? If the plan depends on someone "figuring it out," mark it down.
- **Quote:** "there's a difference between uh short-term discomfort and long-term harm" (S032)
- **Also:** "five years later it is my most valuable skill set for this company" (S032)
- **Sources:** 6 (S011, S016, S032, S036, S041, S058)

### 16. Praise over punishment (reinforcement and the recognition scrambler)
- **What it is:** Reinforce the behavior you want; punishment gets minimum effort and only works while the boss is watching. Consequences are not punishment: they are facts agreed up front.
- **Parts:** Costs of punishment: secrecy, anxiety, resentment, no discretionary effort, no innovation, and overstaffing because people only work as high as the bar (S054). Consequences: inform calmly and let the person choose ("you can also not work here, so which one do you pick") (S007, S025, S054). Negative reinforcement only for immoral, unethical, or illegal acts (S007). Recognition scrambler: pick a when (outcome, effort, milestone, values), a who (manager, company, peer), a what (formal or spontaneous), and a how (written, verbal, reward) (S044). Her employee brand promise at Acquisition.com: praise over punishment (S050, S010).
- **When to use:** Low morale, high churn, a manager who yells, or a team that does the minimum.
- **How to score:** Does the plan reward the behavior it wants more of, weekly? A plan that relies on threat scores low and predicts churn.
- **Quote:** "If they only do things out of threat of losing their job then they will give you the minimum effort required to maintain the job." (S012)
- **Also:** "There's a difference between punishment and consequences, the reality is is if you don't do your job that we pay you for you won't have a job." (S054)
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S010, S012, S019, S025, S044, S050, S054)

## Get the founder out of the way

### 17. Delegation levels: demonstrate, document, duplicate
- **What it is:** Delegation is not all or nothing; it is "to what extent," moved up step by step as trust is earned.
- **Parts:** Four levels: investigation (they research, you act), informed progress (they own it with updates), informed results (they report only the result), complete ownership (S035). The biggest teaching happens in the gap between levels 2 and 3 (S035). Five-level accountability dial: told what to do, does tasks, does and gets feedback before finishing, decides and gets feedback after, decides with no loop-in (S060). Demonstrate, document, duplicate: show them, they write the SOP from watching, they repeat until they hit your standard (her 80% close rate was the bar for a new rep) (S012). The Q&A step: bring the question and the answer you think I'd give; after about 10 right answers, go do it yourself (S060). Transfer judgment: when a leader asks her to handle their hard call, she helps script it instead (S011).
- **When to use:** Founder bottlenecks, a new leader, anything the founder is "still doing."
- **How to score:** For each task the founder holds, name the level it is at and the date it moves up. Tasks with no named next level score against.
- **Quote:** "it's not all or nothing it's not delegate or don't delegate it's delegate to what extent" (S035)
- **Also:** "The person who documents the process learns the most so why would you keep that to yourself by being the one to document it." (S012)
- **Sources:** 7 (S011, S012, S017, S021, S035, S053, S060)

### 18. What to keep, what to give away
- **What it is:** A sort for founder time: keep the high-value, hard-to-reverse calls; give away the rest, cheapest first.
- **Parts:** Quadrant of suck at, dislike, good at, like, layered with dollars per hour: delegate what you suck at, dislike, and is cheap to hire first, then low-value work you are good at; keep high-value work you like (S012). Exception: important, hard to reverse, high-dollar decisions stay with you even if you dislike them, such as firing (S012). Eisenhower matrix for the CEO: her lane is big, important, urgent, irreversible problems; anything under $10,000 or hiring non-managers is not (S007). Know your hourly value and stop doing anything below it (S004). The founder's time goes to the biggest constraint and to pulling the future into the present (S019, S020). Theme your days: meeting days, deep work days, content days (S008).
- **When to use:** A founder working 70-hour weeks, a calendar full of tasks, or "I need an EA."
- **How to score:** What share of the founder's week is spent on the top constraint and on decisions only they can make? Under half is a red flag.
- **Quote:** "If there's something very important and is very hard to reverse and has a very high dollar amount tied to it even if I don't like doing it probably going to do it." (S012)
- **Also:** "what's the most important and Urgent problem in the company and then I go work on that" (S007)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S007, S008, S012, S019, S020, S037, S018)

### 19. The right operator for the stage
- **What it is:** Most stuck $3M to $20M businesses she reviews have an operator problem. The right operator speeds the business up and frees about 70% of the founder's time; the wrong one slows it down.
- **Parts:** Hire the operator for your weaknesses and dislikes (S028). Builder versus incremental improver: builders for $0 to $10M and again past $50M; improvers for $10M to $50M professionalization (S028). Stage roles: EA or ops coordinator to $1M, ops manager to $10M, VP or COO beyond (S028). Decide if you need someone to manage the day to day or to manage and grow, now and in a year; do not hire a manager and blame them for not growing the company (S019, S055). An operator who is great at $3M and $100M is rare; expect a season (S018). Zero tolerance for an operator who undermines the founder (S028). She runs each new business herself for the first 12 to 18 months before handing off (S028).
- **When to use:** "Should I hire a COO or integrator?" or a business that stalled after a key hire.
- **How to score:** Check the month the operator was hired against the month growth stalled. Is the operator tied to revenue?
- **Quote:** "if your operator's not a 10 out of 10 your business isn't going to be a 10 out of 10. it's that simple" (S028)
- **Also:** "a lot of the times the month that they hire them is the month that their business puts stagnant that's not a coincidence" (S028)
- **Sources:** 6 (S018, S019, S028, S047, S055, S063)

### 20. Stage map: the 10 levels and the founder's changing job
- **What it is:** Each stage has its own constraint and trap, and the founder's job changes from doer to manager to operator to architect.
- **Parts:** 10 levels from improvise (no clear thing to sell) to institutionalize (250 to 500 people) (S027). Key bands: under 10 people, document repeatable processes; 20 to 49, onboarding, a real monthly P&L, role clarity; 50 to 99, HR systems, directors above managers; 100 to 250, specialists and executives who own P&Ls, where the founder stops managing people and manages the systems that manage them (S027). Revenue version: one product, one avatar, one channel to $100K; consistency to $1M; get others to be consistent to $10M (S001); past $10M it becomes a leadership problem (S060). At about 10 people leadership ability becomes the problem or the solution (S021). Systems last about 18 months before they need replacing (S027).
- **When to use:** Any "what should we do next" question, or advice borrowed from a company of a different size.
- **How to score:** Name the level. Does the proposal fix that level's constraint, or a later level's? Doing the right thing in the wrong order scores low.
- **Quote:** "you are going to stop managing people and you have to start managing the systems that manage the people." (S027)
- **Also:** "It's like literally at like 10 people is where leadership ability becomes a problem or a solution for people" (S021)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S021, S027, S028, S060, S009)

### 21. Management bandwidth and adding a layer
- **What it is:** Before adding people or promoting, check how many people you can actually help succeed, and add senior layers slowly.
- **Parts:** Ask what kind of people can succeed under you and how much help you can give (S016). Ten direct reports is "pushing it": use shorter, more frequent one-on-ones (S018). Promote people who already act like leaders; name the shift from buddy to boss openly (S053). Transition existing managers under a new senior leader one at a time over a quarter, with overlap and check-ins (S018). Players promoted to coach must stay off the court and accept a delayed reward (S024, S023). Under $10M, hire a player-coach, not a pure coach (S063).
- **When to use:** Promotions, a new executive layer, or a manager drowning in reports.
- **How to score:** Count direct reports per leader and whether the new leader has led at this size. A first-time manager over 10 people scores low.
- **Quote:** "What's my management bandwidth? Like what kind of people can succeed under me?" (S016)
- **Also:** "I'm not going to promote somebody and wish and hope they start acting like a leader, I promote somebody because they already act like one." (S053)
- **Sources:** 6 (S016, S018, S023, S024, S053, S063)

## Let people go

### 22. Should I fire them? Diagnose first, then up or out
- **What it is:** Check your own part before acting, then coach up or coach out, fast enough to protect the team.
- **Parts:** Four reasons: missing standards, tearing down the team, apathy, drama; each with a self-check (did you set metrics and train them, did you filter for culture, did you tell them directly) (S036). To what extent is it my fault, their fault, or the situation (S036)? Right person in the wrong seat, wrong season of life, or wrong boss: move, reshape, or re-report before firing a good person (S061). Enforcement matrix: high skill and values, promote; high values, low skill, coach; high skill, low values, usually coach out; low and low, out now (S060). Grace budget: can the business withstand this much grace (S061)? Rate of improvement is the signal (S052, S019). Know your pattern: too fast or too slow (S036).
- **When to use:** Any underperformer, any toxic high performer.
- **How to score:** Has the person heard it plainly, with a plan and dates? If yes and no improvement, move. If a high performer violates values, keeping them scores against the whole team.
- **Quote:** "My outcome is either I coach them up and they rise to the occasion or I coach them out" (S036)
- **Also:** "Now your whole team thinks that behavior matters less than output." (S060)
- **Sources:** 9 (S019, S036, S041, S047, S052, S060, S061, S062, S010)

### 23. The exit and layoff playbook
- **What it is:** How you let people go is what the rest of the team watches. Do it with process, honesty, and more generosity than feels comfortable.
- **Parts:** Nobody is fired without knowing what they did wrong, except illegal or crazy behavior (S041). Remove system access during the conversation (S010). Layoffs: let the manager go first if they caused it, meet each person individually, give severance beyond your comfort, line up interviews and write recommendation letters, take full blame, tell the remaining team the truth, expect people to be shaken for up to a year (S020). Help mishires land elsewhere (S010). Do not invent work for surplus people (S035, S058). If an ex-employee retaliates, do not retaliate (S010).
- **When to use:** Firings, layoffs, shutting a department.
- **How to score:** Does the plan protect the people leaving and tell the people staying the truth? A quiet, unexplained exit scores against trust.
- **Quote:** "the way that you treat somebody on the way out is all that your team's going to be paying attention to" (S041)
- **Also:** "you don't find work for people like that that's something i know" (S035)
- **Sources:** 6 (S010, S020, S035, S041, S058, S036)

## Decide

### 24. Decision filters: rushed or emotional, every day, the four multipliers
- **What it is:** Her tools for making the call, and making it at the right speed.
- **Parts:** Am I rushed or emotional? If so, wait (S034). The every-day test: if I made this kind of decision every day for the rest of my life, what would my life look like (S034)? Ten minutes and ten years: better in both (S015). Four multipliers: reach (how many execute it without you), repetition (how many reuse the reasoning), duration, and reversibility, which she calls the tax on decision-making (S062). Create more options than keep or kill (coach, move roles, change boss) (S062). Deadlines and a decision mode, batched weekly (S062). Execution or strategy failure? If the strategy was right, try again (S005). Logical decisions usually feel like nothing (S034). Four bad-decision drivers: boredom, anxiety, distraction, compulsion (S034).
- **When to use:** Any big, hard-to-reverse call: hires, partnerships, investments, new product lines.
- **How to score:** Was it decided calm, with at least three options, and does the plan say how reversible it is? High reach and low reversibility demand more thought; easy reversals should just be tested.
- **Quote:** "am i rushed or am i emotional if i'm either of those things then i don't make a decision i just won't" (S034)
- **Also:** "There's no amount of execution that can out execute an entire team executing on your one decision that you made" (S021)
- **Sources:** 7 (S005, S015, S021, S034, S052, S062, S018)
