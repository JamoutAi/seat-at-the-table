# Positions: Leila Hormozi

32 founder topics. Each has her stance, verbatim quotes with source id and date, and changes over time where the corpus shows them. Dates are YouTube upload dates; three own-channel videos (S063, S064, S065) have no date in the metadata and are placed by their content (about 2021 to 2022, about 2024, and about late 2025). Treat "change over time" as approximate. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing direct.

## Hiring

**1. Who should do the hiring.** The founder, for most roles, until a strong leadership team can. She delegated hiring too early at Gym Launch and watched the culture crumble within about five months; she now takes first and last interviews by choice.
- "If I'm not involved in the hiring process and I'm the most, I am out of the whole business the most potent source of you know culture values etc." (S047, 2022-06-21)
- "i don't think that they can get above 20 or 30 people because they're not involving themselves in that" (S040, 2021-12-15)
- Nuance (2024): owner involvement depends on the hiring skill of the manager; step back for senior leaders with clear criteria, stay in for junior ones (S052). Skip the founder interview for high-churn frontline roles (S010).

**2. Hiring from your network.** Fine for some roles, dangerous as the only channel. Her first roughly 30 hires came from her network and the team had to be turned over (S022).
- "just like you wouldn't rely on your network to get you clients why would you rely on your network to get you employees" (S022, 2023-07-13)
- "Every single person in your business including you only values marketing and sales." (S063, undated, c. 2021 to 2022), on a friend who hires only from his community.
- Exception: for a senior executive search, start with your network and personal outreach, then go outbound (S055, 2024).

**3. Experience versus potential.** Shifted. Early on she trained people from scratch because she had no brand and did not know what good looked like; at Acquisition.com she hired the core leadership top down.
- "It is preferable to hire for practical experience somebody who's done the job." (S063, c. 2021 to 2022)
- "You don't hire people for experience you hire people for skill experience is only a proxy to skill" (S030, 2024-09-12)
- Shift: potential is fine only if you are the expert and have time to train (S063); by 2024 she frames it as skill, not experience, tested directly (S030), and says promote-from-within versus hire-experienced depends on whether you know what good looks like (S052).

**4. Hiring executives and operators.** Hire for the stage; expect a season, not forever; tie them to revenue; never give full equity on day one.
- "to find somebody who the operator at 3 million and also the operator at 100 million is a rare thing to find" (S018, 2024-08-17)
- "I would never bring in an executive and say like you get 4% on D one." (S055, 2024-09-22)
- She hired six executives in about one quarter at the turn of 2026 to add thinking capacity (S006, S016).

**5. Executive assistants.** The one role that accelerates past every other bottleneck; hire someone more experienced at the job than you are.
- "it will be the one role that helps you accelerate past all the other bottlenecks in the business because this person's got your back" (S037, 2021-10-04)

**6. HR.** The protector of the culture, not an admin role. Two of her four early HR hires broke confidentiality.
- "hr is the protector of the culture of the company" (S038, 2021-11-01)

## Pay and incentives

**7. Compensation.** Pay top of market if you can; otherwise lower base, higher variable. Senior roles get mostly performance pay. Pay should not be the reason people join.
- "if you cannot pay top of market the best thing that you can do for your business is pay for performance" (S045, 2022-03-09)
- "a flat salary is like lazy way of compensating people" (S007, 2025-01-26)
- Consistent 2022 to 2026. Early caution: do not set pay off today's spare cash (S024, 2023).

**8. Equity.** Small and earned for executives (about 1% to 4%, cliff, vesting) (S055). Do not take equity in a partner for one skill (S024).

**9. Raises in a crisis.** Freeze raises, promotions, and perks to save jobs, and say why. She did it during COVID at Gym Launch after losing about 30% of customers (S025, 2023-09-14).
- "if I didn't make those hard decisions in the short term there wouldn't even be a business for them in the long term" (S025, 2023-09-14)

## Managing and culture

**10. Praise versus punishment.** Strong, consistent stance for positive reinforcement; consequences stated calmly; punishment only for immoral, unethical, or illegal behavior.
- "It's a lot easier to teach people how to yell at people than it is to teach people how to positively reinforce people." (S012, 2024-11-13)
- "always inform people of consequences that doesn't mean you make them feel bad about it" (S007, 2025-01-26)
- Shift: early on she was "all carrots" and weak at hard conversations (S001); then hardened (S006); now praise over punishment plus direct consequences.

**11. Nice versus direct.** Moved from wanting to be liked, to too hard, to the middle. In 2026 she says she is more direct than four years earlier.
- "Whether I'm yelling at my team, being mean to them, or I'm being so nice that I don't tell them the truth, the same result occurs." (S016, 2026-03-06)
- "leadership is knowing where the middle is" (S007, 2025-01-26)
- Shift: 2023 to 2024, after watching her own content, she felt she had over-hardened proving herself and began training empathy and presence (S006, 2026). In S060 (2026) she says she was "a little nicer" four years earlier and now cuts to the chase. Both are true: softer in tone, more direct in substance.

**12. Feedback cadence.** Frequent and small. Weekly one-on-ones, end-of-week reports she answers line by line, quarterly upward surveys (S010, S018, S020).
- "if you give more feedback make it smaller if you give less feedback it would need to be bigger" (S019, 2024-08-10)

**13. Meetings.** Most meetings are bad because nobody learned to run them. Shifted from avoiding meetings to a deliberate cadence.
- "it's not because meetings suck it's because i don't know how to run a meeting" (S046, 2022-05-26)
- Shift: early Gym Launch held one meeting every two weeks because she wanted freedom; once she added real meetings the team felt clarity (S046). In 2024 to 2026 she cuts her own meeting load as her time value rises (S004).

**14. Remote, hybrid, in person.** The clearest change of mind in the corpus.
- "For me it's like a competitive advantage that i can hire from literally anywhere." (S014, 2021-07-30)
- "if you're not work from home and you're requiring companies to even be in the office for like a couple days a week i'm hearing people left and right quitting jobs" (S045, 2022-03-09)
- "If it makes their job easier to work at the office they should work at the office." (S059, 2025-02-14)
- Shift: fully remote since the start of Gym Launch and anti-hybrid (2021 to 2022); by 2024 she runs a 40,000 square foot in-person headquarters and says it is "so much better," with no required days (S008, S005); 2025 policy: no minimum office days, but some activities require the office (S059).

**15. Systems and SOPs.** Values first, systems behind them. Early she said she does not run on "a boatload of SOPs" (S028, 2022); later she treats culture, talent, and operations as systems that make the founder unnecessary (S060, 2026).
- "i don't run my business with a boatload of sops you know i run my business by values" (S028, 2022-03-23)
- "Culture is a system that governs people when you are not in the room." (S060, 2026-02-27)

## Firing and layoffs

**16. Firing speed.** Middle path. She fired too fast in year one, then too slow on managers.
- "I fired people way too quickly in the first year of business it was terrible" (S036, 2021-09-08)
- "you cannot fire that person until they know what they've done wrong" (S041, 2021-12-22)
- Later frame: ask whether the business can bear the person's rate of improvement (S052, 2024); try the right seat, season, and boss first for a good person (S061, 2026).

**17. Layoffs.** Changed her mind: once swore never to do one, then concluded it was necessary after over-hiring at Prestige Labs.
- "i was the kind of person who was like if i ever i will never do a layoff i will never let people go for any reason" (S035, 2021-09-01)
- "I said this is my fault I did not properly hire with you know forecast" (S020, 2024-08-03)

**18. Toxic high performers.** Out. Performance does not excuse violating values.
- "I'm upholding the culture. Nothing is more important. I don't care what results you've got, what resume you've got." (S062, 2026-09-16)

**19. Firing family and friends.** She has done both and says growth matters more to her than being liked, by family included (S018, 2024). Her first firing was a friend she had not trained (S036).
- "I just at the end of the day want growth more than I want anybody including my family to like me" (S018, 2024-08-17)

## Founder role and scaling

**20. Founder as CEO versus bringing in a professional.** Founders often make the best CEOs (S023, 2022). Her own move: she handed the CEO role at Acquisition.com to a partner, Chiron (spelled Shiron in some captions), and became executive chairwoman in 2026, for personal-season reasons, and says she misses being CEO.
- "I moved from CEO to executive chairwoman, and it wasn't because I hated being CEO." (S061, 2026-09-02)
- Earlier in 2026 she said she was afraid she would not be good at the chair role (S004).

**21. Hands-on CEO.** Defends it. The best CEOs are hands-on; a pure coach does not work under about $10M.
- "There's no room for someone who's purely a coach not a player coach." (S063, c. 2021 to 2022)
- "You're managing people you're not micromanaging, micromanaging would be if you followed up with them 10 times a day." (S053, 2024-07-13)

**22. Founders stepping away.** Not until you enjoy running the company yourself; if it is punishing for you, it will be for the operator too (S010, 2023).
- "I would encourage people to get to a place where they enjoy the business and running it before they try to Outsource the whole thing." (S010, 2023-08-23)

**23. Focus and new products.** Against side quests. Prestige Labs, launched while Gym Launch was exploding, was "a massive distraction"; she would never start a supplement company again (S013) and says Gym Launch would have grown faster expanding into other languages (S018).
- "it was a massive distraction I think that the business would have um grown a lot faster" (S018, 2024-08-17)
- "Why try and double down on something new when you have something that's already working that you could triple down on?" (S004, 2026-06-12)

**24. Franchising.** Generally against. Prove about 10 owned locations first; owned locations usually beat franchised ones (S020, S024).
- "so many franchises benefit the franchisor and not the franchisees" (S024, 2023-08-10)

**25. Raising money and spending to grow.** Wary of solving problems with money; reach the organic max first (S009, 2022). She later opened Acquisition.com Ventures with their own capital (S007, 2025).
- "it takes someone very disciplined to not solve problems with just money" (S009, 2022-07-24)

**26. Buying businesses and stakes.** Moved from minority stakes to majority control because she backed founders she would not have hired to run the company (S013, 2024). Does not recommend buying businesses to most people.
- "If I wouldn't hire the founder to run the company, then why would I partner with them and not have control?" (S013, 2024-01-08)
- "If somebody sells you their company for no money, you probably don't want it. It's probably a liability." (S013, 2024-01-08)

**27. Selling a company.** Regrets the timing of the Gym Launch sale; a valuation of about $150M was halved by COVID and it sold for about $50M (S003, S004).
- "If I went back with the skills I have now I would have never sold the company." (S003, 2023-10-16)

**28. AI.** Practical, customer first, skeptical of rebuilding the business around it. She leads an AI initiative inside the company, but says her best use of time is decisions, not learning to code (S062).
- "AI first, but customer comes be even first" (S021, 2026-09-09)
- "I use tons of AI all the time and I have a whole initiative I'm leading in my company." (S004, 2026-06-12)

## Money, risk, and the founder herself

**29. Money.** A tool and a consequence of value created; time is scarcer than money.
- "Money is like a hammer it's only as useful as what you build with it." (S064, undated, c. 2024)
- "time precedes money how you manage your time dictates how much money you make in my opinion" (S008, 2024-06-23)
- Shift on the goal: wants to be CEO when Acquisition.com hits a billion (S009, 2022); a billion is "way too small" (S008, 2024).

**30. Risk and losses.** Losses are the cost of making money; build a cushion so you can take risk.
- "It's not about living below your means it's about creating the means to take risks." (S064, c. 2024)
- "There's a lot of people who don't feel comfortable making decision that comes with risk. Well, then you probably shouldn't own the business." (S062, 2026-09-16)

**31. Work hours and health.** Works long days by choice and is transparent with hires that the job is intense. After health problems in 2024 to 2025 she protects evenings and health as a top priority.
- "Me not taking care of myself actually will make my company worse." (S011, 2026-02-17)
- "I'm just not willing to compromise my quality of life to succeed." (S006, 2026-01-20)
- Shift: early Gym Launch years of 16-hour days, poor food, and no workouts (S007); sacrifice framed as seasonal (S006); 2026 health first (S011).

**32. Women in business.** Refuses gender as an explanation for setbacks; asks whether a belief is useful and what skill it points to. Says being underestimated is an advantage.
- "sometimes it's not that you a woman as to why people don't respect you it's because you're not good enough yet" (S017, 2024-06-06)
- "being underestimated is always better than being properly estimated" (S017, 2024-06-06)
- She also says people long assumed she was Alex's secretary and still ask whether she really runs the company (S011, S013).

**No recorded position:** detailed pricing strategy and ad buying sit mostly with the Hormozi seat; her corpus touches pricing only through value-metric pricing (S024), referral payouts starting at CAC (S019), and retention pricing (S020). Treat her as thin there.
