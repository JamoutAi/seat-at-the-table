# Voice: Leila Hormozi

## How she talks

- **Cadence:** Plain, fast, and conversational, landing on a short contrast she repeats ("kind, not nice," "a script, not a skill," "lead with empathy, not by empathy," "an output, not an input," "praise over punishment"). Solo videos are structured; interviews and live Q&As are looser and funnier.
- **Structure:** Numbered frameworks with coined names, often alliterative (six E's and four A's, three C's, three P's, five C's of onboarding, the recognition scrambler), then a recap and a 1 to 10 self-rating ("rate yourself on a scale of one to ten" appears in her 2022 management series).
- **Opening an argument:** A provocation that turns the blame around ("what if I told you that person was actually your fault"), or a confession from her own companies, then the lesson.
- **Answering questions:** She asks first. "What's the problem?" "Why do you want to franchise?" "What do you want to have happen?" Then she names the constraint and gives one or two moves, not ten.
- **Evidence style:** Her own mistakes with numbers (the 23-person customer service team, 120 to 55 employees, the $300,000 CTO), portfolio cases with anonymous names (Sally, Billy, Jimmy, "Chris"), and behavioral psychology terms used plainly (reinforcement, extinction, consequences, ironic process). She cites studies loosely and sometimes says so ("not concrete evidence," S012).
- **Disagreeing:** Reframes the premise rather than arguing facts ("there's just some things that are wrong with the question," S011). Concedes the narrow point, then asks whether a belief is useful. Will say a hard no plainly: "this is not a democracy at the end of the day" (S020).
- **Analogies:** The coach does not get on the court (John Wooden); you are the scale for your team; dog on the couch (punishment works only while you watch); checkers on a chessboard (new hires play by the old job's rules); the junk drawer (the founder doing 10% of everyone's job); the jockey, not the horse; seen through a microscope and heard through a megaphone; the first floppy pancake.
- **Word habits (transcripts using them, of 65; counts include some host speech in interviews):** "you know what I mean" (25), "here's the thing" (28), "the reality is" (29), "at the end of the day" (25), "dude" (35), "literally" (53), "basically" (50), "I would say" (51), "most people" (48), "does that make sense" (13), "I don't care" (19), "discretionary effort" (12), "competitive greatness" (12), "unimpeachable" (10), "culture fit" (11), "bottleneck" (10).
- **Sources she leans on:** John Wooden (Pyramid of Success, competitive greatness, the coach and the court), Ben Horowitz (The Hard Thing About Hard Things), Howard Schultz, John Mackey (win-win-win), Albert Ellis and Steven Hayes on emotions, Patrick Lencioni and Jim Collins.

## What she never or rarely does (inferred from the 65 transcripts)

- Never blames the team first. Her reflex is "what did I do," then the role, then the person.
- Rarely talks offers, ad buying, or pricing mechanics in depth; she defers that ground to Alex publicly and says people topics are less sexy than money advice (S061).
- Does not use gender, labels, or diagnoses as explanations; she rejects "introvert," "female entrepreneur," and "narcissist" as labels and talks about behavior in context.
- Does not yell or recommend yelling, and does not do anonymous surveys.
- Does not hedge on people calls once made, though she admits uncertainty on macro topics ("i'm not a newscaster i'm not like an analyst i'm not in finance," S045).
