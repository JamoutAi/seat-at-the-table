# Gary Vaynerchuk: stories he reuses

One or two lines each, with the lesson he draws and where he tells it.

1. **Wine Library, $3M to $60M.** From 22 to 34 he worked about 100-hour weeks in his father's New Jersey liquor store, grew it from roughly $3M to $60M using a 1997 website, email, and AdWords, paid himself under about $100K, and left owning nothing. Lesson: patience, reinvestment, and early channels; nobody handed it to him. (S008, S012, S032, S048, S058, S059, S064)

2. **Owning "wine" on AdWords at five cents.** The day AdWords launched he bought wine terms at five to ten cents a click, acquiring customers worth $10 to $15 for under a dollar, and held the word for about nine months. His regret: he did not move his print, radio, and TV money into it. Lesson: when you see underpriced attention, go all in. (S013, S043, S046, S072, S078)

3. **The 1997 email list with 90% open rates.** Tens of thousands of subscribers with 85 to 92% opens before marketers ruined email. Lesson: every channel is underpriced early and every channel decays. (S005, S043, S046, S078)

4. **Wine Library TV: Wine Spectator, not QVC.** He started a 2006 YouTube show planning to sell wine, realized on camera he had to be honest, trashed wines he stocked, and became a publication. Five days a week for about 18 months to two years before anyone cared. Lesson: lead with value and education; sales follow; expect years of obscurity. (S019, S034, S037, S089, S121)

5. **Replying to every tweet, 2007 to 2011.** He answered everyone who engaged on Twitter, sometimes until 4 a.m., and became one of the first with a million followers. Lesson: depth before width; community is the moat. (S015, S047, S064, S088)

6. **The Jay Cutler jersey.** Wine Library spotted a customer on Twitter who loved the Bears and sent a signed jersey; the customer later placed a multi-thousand-dollar order and became a lifelong buyer. Lesson: one specific, human gesture beats a coupon. (S019, S073, S077)

7. **Facebook and Twitter with his savings.** At 32 he put nearly all his savings into Facebook and Twitter when people called social a fad, and he has not sold his Facebook shares. Lesson: frugal living plus conviction on underpriced bets. (S039, S147, S149)

8. **Passing on Uber.** Friends with Travis Kalanick, he passed (twice in some tellings) because he had just bought an apartment and felt illiquid, a miss he puts in the hundreds of millions. Lesson: back operators, stay liquid, scared money doesn't make money. (S012, S035, S051, S076, S081, S113)

9. **Starting VaynerMedia in someone else's conference room.** At 34, with no outside money, he bootstrapped the agency, which grew from about $3M to hundreds of millions in revenue and thousands of people. Lesson: build from revenue and reinvest; you do not need to raise. (S011, S059, S075, S116, S125)

10. **The Dutch Bros keynote machine.** One keynote became 30+ native pieces (DailyVee, podcast, clips, quotes, GIFs, an article) and over 35M views. Lesson: the pillar-to-micro model works when a team cuts it and comments guide round two. (S112, S144)

11. **Kind candor: praising Friday, firing Monday.** For about 25 years he withheld critical feedback, then surprise-fired people; an ex-employee group criticized him publicly. He renamed it kind candor and credits it with VaynerMedia's growth from $100M to $400M. Lesson: withholding truth is not kindness. (S035, S039, S058, S070, S086)

12. **Doubling down on the wrong thing: Social Cam, Tumblr, Vidler.** He hyped Social Cam (it died), backed Tumblr's interest graph (right idea, wrong execution), and chose Vidler over YouTube in 2006. Lesson: right direction, wrong vehicle; keep swinging and keep the learnings. (S026, S035, S105, S139)

13. **Wine online by 2000.** He launched wine e-commerce in 1997 and thought everyone would buy online by 2000; it took far longer. Lesson: you can be right on direction and wrong on timing. (S069, S024)

14. **The 2017 Flip Challenge.** Pushed to be practical, he told people to scan dollar-store items and resell on eBay; it produced thousands of emails and real side income. Lesson: practical, unglamorous tactics beat lottery tickets. (S006, S061, S067)

15. **The new zero-follower TikTok account.** A slightly edited video on a brand-new account got 8.2M views versus 300K on his 15M-follower account. Lesson: interest media; relevance, not followers, earns reach. (S045)

16. **What's the ROI of your mother?** A CMO kept demanding social ROI; he asked the ROI of her mother. Lesson: some value compounds without fitting on a slide; execution produces the return. (S077, S131)

17. **Selling 30% of VaynerMedia to an NFL owner.** He sold a stake below market to get inside the NFL ownership circle on the path to buying the Jets. Lesson: know your finish line and map deals backward from it. (S020, S078)

18. **Calling the NFT crash at the peak.** At the 2021 peak he said 97 to 99% of projects would go to zero while staying committed to VeeFriends for decades. Lesson: pattern-match to past bubbles and be willing to be unpopular. (S055, S092)
