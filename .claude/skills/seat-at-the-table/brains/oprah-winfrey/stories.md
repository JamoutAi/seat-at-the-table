# Stories: Oprah Winfrey

18 stories and case studies she reuses, most-told first. Details are as she tells them; numbers and dates sometimes vary between tellings.

1. **The Klan show and the mistress reveal (around 1988 to 1989).** She booked Klan members and skinheads to "expose" them, then saw them signaling each other during a commercial break and realized they were using her show to recruit. Soon after, a husband told his wife on live TV that his mistress was pregnant; she saw the wife's humiliation and said never again. She told the producers they would only do intentional shows, held pre- and post-show meetings on intention, and credits this shift with 25 years at number one. Lesson: know whose agenda your platform serves; use it, don't be used by it. (S001, S002, S004, S010, S011, S012, S018, S021, S023, S026, S027)

2. **The Color Purple contract and owning herself (1985 to 1986).** Her Chicago bosses gave her two weeks of vacation a year; she needed two months to film *The Color Purple*, so she gave up all her vacation for the rest of the contract. At renewal her lawyer Jeff Jacobs said she should never again be unable to do what mattered to her, and asked if she wanted to own herself: no salary, take the risk, keep what the show earned. She took it, starting at 50% and ending at 93%. Lesson: own yourself; take less now to get more later. (S003, S007, S021, S022, S040)

3. **OWN's rough start and turnaround (2011 to 2013).** She launched the network while ending the show, assumed her old audience would follow (many did not have cable or the OWN package), had no right full-time leader, and spent 2012 reading headlines about the "struggling" network, including USA Today's "Oprah not quite standing on her OWN." She laid off 30 people, rebuilt one night of programming at a time, prayed for a way, and two days later Tyler Perry offered a scripted drama. She later added Ava DuVernay's *Queen Sugar*. She told Harvard in 2013 she had turned it around. Lesson: serve the audience you actually have, get the right leader, and take the next right move. (S002, S010, S011, S021, S023, S032, S033, S038, S055, S056)

4. **The decision she knew was wrong (2010 to 2011).** Years later she says the voice inside her said not to launch then; she woke at night unable to breathe, but had signed the contracts and felt she had to keep her word, mostly to avoid what people would think. Everyone said to leverage the moment. She calls it her deepest regret and her clearest example of not listening to herself. Lesson: you can change your mind; wait when you are not sure. (S021, S023, S055, S056)

5. **Sandra in the Kroger frozen food aisle (1990s).** A viewer told her she stopped hitting her son because Oprah said, every time the topic came up, that you should not beat your kids. "You were consistent." This is the moment she accepted being a brand: a brand is consistency over time. (S010)

6. **The letter from Carol in Ann Arbor (around 1987).** A viewer wrote that watching Oprah be herself made her want to be more of herself. She framed the letter and says it is what she would put on her tombstone. Lesson: real connection is people becoming more themselves, not more like you. (S010, S020)

7. **The talk show demotion in Baltimore (1976 to 1978).** As a news anchor she felt unnatural, cried for people in her stories, and was told she was too emotional. They wanted to rename her Suzie and sent her to a salon where a perm made her hair fall out. To run out her contract, they put her on a talk show; the first day, interviewing the Carvel ice cream man, it felt like breathing. Lesson: a setback can be redirection; know what you do not want. (S001, S004, S008, S009, S012)

8. **Moving to Chicago against everyone's advice (1984).** Everyone except Gayle said she would fail against Phil Donahue in a racist city. She turned down a car and apartment to stay in Baltimore because it felt right; she beat Donahue in her first month locally. Lesson: follow the feeling, and prepare so you are ready when the moment comes. (S001, S027, S045)

9. **The daily focus group (from about the tenth season).** She stopped signing autographs after tapings and started talking with the audience for 30 to 45 minutes a day, asking what they really wanted. She calls it the reason the show stayed number one. (S019, S020, S026, S034, S035)

10. **Saying no to Stevie Wonder.** As a self-described people pleaser being asked for money constantly, she tested the principle of intention by saying no to Stevie Wonder's charity request. He said OK. Lesson: people follow the intention of your yes; a no from clarity rarely costs what you fear. (S003)

11. **The episode she would not air (2010).** Her team taped a woman who was a Sunday school teacher by day and a sex addict at night, who consented to everything. When Oprah learned she had a 10-year-old son, she killed the episode. Lesson: the last vote is about who gets hurt, even if they are not in the room. (S006)

12. **The producers' raise (1986).** When the show went national she got a raise but her all-female producers did not; her boss asked why, since they were "only girls." She said they get raises or she would not work. Lesson: use your leverage for the people under you. (S014)

13. **The beef trial (1998).** Texas cattlemen sued her over a mad cow remark; she spent six weeks in court, met Dr. Phil there, and kept asking what the trial was there to teach her. Lesson: every trial has a lesson, including respect for the reach of your words. (S010)

14. **Beloved bombs (1998).** She worked ten years on the film adaptation of Toni Morrison's *Beloved*; it flopped, she fell into a depression and ate macaroni and cheese for breakfast, then decided to detach from outcomes and treat her work as an offering. (S018)

15. **The magazine that made her overconfident (2000 onward).** She was told O magazine would take five years to profit; it was profitable in its first month. So she assumed the network would be as easy. It was not. (S033)

16. **Building the Leadership Academy from the outside in (2007).** She checked every blueprint, pillow, and thread count of her girls' school in South Africa, then faced an abuse allegation against a dorm matron. She cried for half an hour, assembled investigators and trauma specialists, flew there, and concluded she had built the school from the outside in when what mattered was the inside out. (S004)

17. **Early philanthropy that failed (1990s).** She tried to move families out of Cabrini Green and started a Big Sister program; both failed. She concluded you must first change how people see themselves, and moved to a strategy that is "emotional and logical": education. (S001, S021)

18. **Maya Angelou on legacy (2007).** When she said the school would be her greatest legacy, Angelou told her that her legacy is every life she has touched, not a name on a building. (S018, S020, S026, S037)
