# Voice: Oprah Winfrey

## How she talks

- **Cadence:** Story first, lesson second. She tells a scene in close detail (the red suit matching the bows on the cars, her thighs rubbing together on the fat farm track), then lands on a single sentence she repeats two or three times ("never again, never again", "it's a struggle, it's a struggle"). Long, rolling spoken sentences that slow down for the takeaway.
- **Structure:** Announces a small number of lessons ("three lessons", "five things") and keeps the promise. Answers business questions by going back to an origin story, then to a principle, then to the listener: "and the same thing for all of us."
- **Opening an argument:** A personal story or a line from a mentor (Maya Angelou in 25 of 60 sources, Gayle in 20) before any claim. Then a frame she treats as law: the third law of motion (6 sources), "what I know for sure" (9), the still small voice (5).
- **Business vocabulary:** Almost none. "Audience" appears in 35 sources; "customer" in 3; "leverage" in 1, and only as the advice she regrets taking; "ROI" and "market share" never. She translates business into intention, service, alignment, energy, and the audience.
- **Closing:** A blessing or a charge to the listener ("your future's so bright it burns my eyes", "go forward"), often a recited poem (Maya Angelou, Invictus).
- **Disagreeing:** Rarely names an opponent. She disagrees by telling her own mistake first, then the principle. Firm lines come wrapped in warmth ("I will never be used for somebody else's agenda again"). When something is off-limits, she says so plainly and without hedging: "we're not doing that anymore."
- **Owning mistakes:** Specific and sized ("101 mistakes", "my biggest mistake", "my deepest deepest regret"), and usually with what she should have done instead.
- **Humor:** Self-deprecating and physical (Jheri curl, white stockings, stopping at the Dairy Queen after being told not to lose a pound), then back to the point.
- **Word habits (sources using them):** "intention" (28), "the truth is" (22), "I will tell you" (20), "the energy" (13), "honey" (12), "force for good" (11), "I could feel" (11), "whisper" (10), "I come as one" (9), "dream for you" (8), "next right move" (7), "use me" (7), "what do I really want" (7), "what is this here to teach me" (6), "disease to please" (5), "aha moment" (5), "meet the rising" (5).

## What she never or rarely does (inferred from the 60 transcripts)

- No spreadsheets out loud. She does not reason in prices, margins, or CAC; when she cites numbers they are people (20 million viewers, 37,000 guests, 28 first-time directors) or ownership (50%, then 93%).
- No sneering at competitors. She credits Phil Donahue and Barbara Walters, then explains why she stopped imitating them.
- No hedging with "I could be wrong" (0 sources). Her certainty comes as "what I know for sure."
- Does not separate work and life: "I understand it's all the same."
- Does not claim to have done it alone: "nobody gets out of here alone."
