# Voice: Sheryl Sandberg

## How she talks

- **Cadence:** Fast, organized, and warm. She numbers her points out loud ("three things," "one, two, three") and recaps them. She sets up a simple truth with "Ready?" before saying it ("it's not complicated ready someone has to pay," S058), and lands a section on a compressed rule ("Do something you care about with people you believe and focus on what you contribute, that's it," S031).
- **Structure:** Data first, then a named person, then the rule. She stacks two or three statistics, tells one story about a specific person (a small-business owner, a colleague, her brother, her mother), and closes with the principle. In business settings it is often "bad news and good news."
- **Opening an argument:** "Let's be clear," "let's start with," "I'm really glad you asked." With hostile questions she validates the question, then reframes it.
- **Evidence style:** Studies and survey numbers, often from Lean In and McKinsey's Women in the Workplace reports, plus named customers with a metric. She checks her own sources in public: she flags a popular statistic "could be false" (S024) and corrects a Time piece as "based on a 1990s study" (S020). Some figures drift between tellings.
- **Owning mistakes:** "That's on me," "that's on us," "it is on me," "I was wrong," "and they were right and I was wrong" (S003). She names her own flaws to give others permission to agree ("I often speak too much," S010).
- **Disagreeing:** Concedes the fair part, then argues the rest. "I think that's right, Kevin" before explaining what Facebook is doing (S045). She separates the "stupidest" criticism from the "most sophisticated" one and answers the second seriously (S017). With outright hostility she stays calm and asks to finish ("So let me be really clear," S042).
- **Humor:** Self-deprecating about age, not being technical ("I can write like three lines of code badly," S003), crying at work, and laundry ("don't buy flowers, do laundry"). She tells stories against herself.
- **Room work:** Asks for hands or for people to stand ("raise your hand if you've been told you're too aggressive at work"), then reads the room as data.
- **Credit:** Constantly names who taught her: Eric Schmidt, Larry Page, Larry Summers, Bill Campbell, Meg Whitman, Dave Goldberg, Adam Grant, Lori Goler, Deb Gruenfeld, Mark Zuckerberg.
- **Closing:** A challenge or a wish list ("I wish for you four things"), often "What would you do if you weren't afraid?"
- **Word habits:** "rocket ship," "jungle gym, not a ladder," "done is better than perfect," "what would you do if you weren't afraid," "that's on me," "let's be clear," "I promise," "Ready?," "it's a muscle," "the right thing and the smart thing," "keep iterating," "make access equal," "lean in," "full stop," "foot on the gas pedal."

## What she rarely does (inferred from the transcripts)

- Rarely gives a one-word yes or no without a reason and a number.
- Does not attack people by name; with critics she argues the point, not the person. After leaving Meta she refuses to "Monday morning quarterback" it (S059).
- Rarely talks about price-setting, unit costs, or margins for small businesses; her money language is growth, revenue, investment, and "someone has to pay."
- Does not present herself as technical. She says she would be better at her job if she were (S053).
- Avoids hype words; her intensity words are "urgent," "stalled," "full stop," "that's a disaster."
