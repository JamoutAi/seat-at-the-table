# Books: Sheryl Sandberg

Built only from how she teaches the books in public (book tours, keynotes, and interviews in the corpus). No book text is reproduced. Two books matter for this seat: Lean In (2013) and Option B (2017, with Adam Grant). She also leans heavily on the annual Women in the Workplace research from LeanIn.Org and McKinsey, which she cites as data, not as a book.

## Lean In: Women, Work, and the Will to Lead (March 2013)

- **Origin:** Her 2010 TEDWomen talk, "Why we have too few women leaders" (S061). She was told speaking about being a woman would end her business career: "You cannot be a serious business executive and speak about being a woman." (S019) Earlier versions of the same three messages appear in her 2009 Stanford talk (S002) and 2011 Barnard commencement (S008), where the phrase "lean in" first shows up.
- **Core thesis:** Progress for women at the top stalled for about a decade. Institutions and policy have to change, and there are also things individuals can do. "The promise of equality is not equality." (S008)
- **Key ideas as she teaches them:** Sit at the table; the success and likability penalty for women (credited to Deb Gruenfeld); men apply when they meet about 60% of the criteria and women wait for 100%; don't leave before you leave; make your partner a real partner; careers are a jungle gym, not a ladder (credited to Lori Goler); "What would you do if you weren't afraid?"; done is better than perfect.
- **Business lens she gives it:** talent supply and performance. "We are still sourcing from half the population" (S014). She tells executives to put bias interrupters into reviews and promotion (frameworks.md #20).
- **The platform:** LeanIn.Org and Lean In Circles, small peer groups that meet monthly. She hoped for 500 to 1,000 circles; counts in the corpus run from 12,000 (S019, early 2014) to 34,000 (S036, 2018) to 155,000 (S060, 2026). She calls circles "book clubs with a purpose" (S015) and argues peer support is the most effective mentoring.
- **What she changed her mind about:**
  - The partner chapter assumed everyone had one: "That chapter said make your partner a real partner so it implied everyone had one and I was wrong" (S055).
  - It told only her story: "By the time we launched I realized the book had only my story and that was a mistake" (S060).
  - From individual to system: the TED talk set aside institutional fixes (S061); by 2025 she says the ambition gap disappears with equal opportunity, "It is the system" (S057).
  - Race: in 2013 she chose not to broaden the book (S016); by 2014 she says "Leadership for a long time has looked white and looked male" (S020) and adds a chapter by Mellody Hobson in the new edition.
- **Criticism she names:** that her advice to women (smile, say "we") reinforces the stereotypes she wants gone. She calls this the most serious criticism and answers that she is "pretty much a pragmatist" (S017). She also concedes the advice is written from privilege.
- **Taught in:** S002, S008, S013, S014, S015, S016, S017, S018, S019, S020, S023, S036, S053, S057, S060, S061, S021.

## Option B: Facing Adversity, Building Resilience, and Finding Joy (April 2017, with Adam Grant)

- **Origin:** Written after her husband Dave Goldberg died suddenly in May 2015. The title comes from a friend, Phil Deutch, who told her "Option A is not available, so let's just kick the shit out of option B." (S025) She first taught the material in her 2016 UC Berkeley commencement speech (S025).
- **Core thesis:** Resilience is not a fixed trait. "You are not born with a fixed amount of resilience. It's a muscle." (S025) People, organizations, and communities can build it.
- **Key ideas as she teaches them:** The three Ps (personalization, pervasiveness, permanence), from Martin Seligman; "it could be worse"; writing down three moments of joy each night; post-traumatic growth and "pre-traumatic growth"; acknowledge the pain rather than avoid it; "do something specific" instead of "let me know if there's anything I can do"; the Platinum Rule (ask what people want); collective resilience. See frameworks.md #24 and #25.
- **Business lens she gives it:** resilient organizations debrief and share failure; managers should offer time off and then praise specific contributions; bereavement and family leave. Facebook moved to 20 days of bereavement leave for an immediate family member and 10 for extended family, and six weeks of paid leave to care for a sick relative (S027, S028, S029). She calls it "pretty much unheard of but pretty much not enough for almost anyone" (S029).
- **Prioritization lesson from it:** After Dave died she cleared her calendar and saw how much she had been doing that she did not need to do. She told Facebook's women to start from nothing and add back only what is critical for work, critical for them, or wanted, while admitting "now granted I'm senior I have a lot more control than a lot of people" (S028).
- **Her caveat:** "I would trade all the growth all the learning all the Gratitude to have Dave back" (S028). She resists tidy uplift and says hardship and resources are unevenly distributed.
- **Platform:** OptionB.org groups; a free COVID mini-version in 2020 (S046).
- **Taught in:** S025, S028, S029, S030, S033, S034, S044, S046, S047, S051, S055, S056, S060.

## Other work she teaches in public

- **Women in the Workplace (LeanIn.Org and McKinsey, annual since 2015):** the "broken rung" (for every 100 men promoted to manager, 93 women and 60 Black women, S057), style-based feedback (66% of women versus 1% of men, S058), and a 2025 finding that about half of companies no longer prioritize women's advancement (S058). Used as her evidence base from 2016 on (S026, S042, S043, S048, S057, S058, S059).
- **Child marriage report (2025, with Hillary Clinton and others):** frames a human-rights issue as an economic one, about $175 billion a year in costs, "this is costing the global economy 175 billion dollars every single year" (S057). An example of her persuasion method: translate the cause into the economics the decision-maker already cares about.
